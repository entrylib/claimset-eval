CLAIMSET Staying Positive: Challenges and Solutions in Using Pure Multiplicative ETS Models
summary Pure multiplicative exponential smoothing (ETS) models are widely used for strictly positive time series, yet standard implementations assume normally distributed errors that can produce negative forecasts and unrealistic prediction intervals on low-volume data. This work shows that conventional point forecasts from pure multiplicative ETS models generally underestimate conditional expectations, with the bias increasing as the damping parameter, trend smoothing parameter, error variance, or forecast horizon grows. To preserve positivity, we formulate pure multiplicative ETS models with Log-Normal, Gamma, and Inverse Gaussian error distributions, deriving their concentrated log-likelihoods, scale estimators, and conditional moments. Simulation experiments and an electric vehicle charging case study demonstrate that information criteria reliably select among these distributions, and that positive distributions prevent negative prediction intervals without requiring transformation to logarithmic ETS models.

DEFINITIONS
D1 [method] ETS(M,*,*): Pure multiplicative exponential smoothing state space model family written in logarithmic state space form as $$\begin{aligned} \log y_t &= w' \log(v_{t-l}) + \log(1 + \epsilon_t) \\ \log v_t &= F \log v_{t-l} + \log(1_k + g \epsilon_t) \end{aligned}$$ with conventional multi-step point forecast $$\hat{y}_{t+h} = \exp\left((w_1' F_1^{h-1} + w_m' F_m^{\lceil h/m \rceil - 1}) \log v_t\right).$$ Here $y_t$ is the actual value on observation $t$; $v_t$ is the state vector, holding the model's components (such as the level, trend and seasonal component); $w$ is the measurement vector; $F$ is the transition matrix; $g$ is the persistence vector, holding the smoothing parameters; $\epsilon_t$ is the error term; $l$ is the lag vector, holding the lag of each component (1 for the level and trend, $m$ for the seasonal component), so that $v_{t-l}$ is the vector of components each taken at its own lag; $1_k$ is the vector of ones of size $k$, the number of components; logarithms and exponents are taken element-wise. In the point forecast, $h$ is the forecast horizon from observation $t$, the subscript $1$ marks the parts of $w$ and $F$ for the non-seasonal components and the subscript $m$ the parts for the seasonal component, $m$ is the seasonal lag, and $\lceil \cdot \rceil$ is the ceiling function. [source B7]
D2 [method] ETS(M,N,N): Pure multiplicative exponential smoothing model with no trend and no seasonality, defined by $$\begin{aligned} y_t &= l_{t-1}(1 + \epsilon_t) \\ l_t &= l_{t-1}(1 + \alpha \epsilon_t) \end{aligned}$$ where $l_t$ is the level, $\alpha \in [0,1]$ is the smoothing parameter, and $1+\epsilon_t$ is the positive multiplicative error term. [a kind of D1] [source B3]
D3 [method] ETS(M,Md,M): Pure multiplicative exponential smoothing model with damped multiplicative trend and multiplicative seasonality, defined by $$\begin{aligned} y_t &= l_{t-1} b_{t-1}^{\phi} s_{t-m} (1 + \epsilon_t) \\ l_t &= l_{t-1} b_{t-1}^{\phi} (1 + \alpha \epsilon_t) \\ b_t &= b_{t-1}^{\phi} (1 + \beta \epsilon_t) \\ s_t &= s_{t-m} (1 + \gamma \epsilon_t) \end{aligned}$$ where $b_t$ is the trend, $s_t$ is the seasonal component, $\phi$ is the dampening parameter, and $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are the smoothing parameters of the trend and the seasonal component. [a kind of D1] [source B3]
D23 [method] ETS(M,M,N): The ETS model with multiplicative error, multiplicative trend and no seasonality.
D22 [data] M3 series N2703: Series N2703 of the M3 forecasting competition, a series with a negative trend. [source B9]
D7 [concept] Normal error distribution ($\mathcal{N}$): Classical Gaussian distribution on $(-\infty, \infty)$ applied to $1+\epsilon_t$ with mean $\mu = 1$ and variance $\sigma^2$. [source B3]
D6 [concept] Inverse Gaussian error distribution ($\mathcal{IG}$): Probability distribution on $(0, \infty)$ applied to $1+\epsilon_t$ with location $\mu = 1$ and scale $\sigma^2$ so that $E(1+\epsilon_t) = 1$ and $V(1+\epsilon_t) = \sigma^2$, where $E$ and $V$ denote expectation and variance.
D4 [concept] Log-Normal error distribution ($\log\mathcal{N}$): Probability distribution on $(0, \infty)$ applied to $1+\epsilon_t$ with location $\mu = -\frac{\sigma^2}{2}$ and scale $\sigma^2$ so that $E(1+\epsilon_t) = 1$ and $V(1+\epsilon_t) = e^{\sigma^2} - 1$, where $\mu$ and $\sigma^2$ are the mean and variance of $\log(1+\epsilon_t)$.
D5 [concept] Gamma error distribution ($\Gamma$): Probability distribution on $(0, \infty)$ applied to $1+\epsilon_t$ with shape $\xi = \sigma^{-2}$ and scale $\sigma^2$ so that $E(1+\epsilon_t) = 1$ and $V(1+\epsilon_t) = \sigma^2$.
D24 [concept] candidate error distributions: The four distributions the paper considers for the multiplicative error term $1+\epsilon_t$ of [[D1:ETS(M,*,*)]]: the [[D7:Normal error distribution]], the [[D6:Inverse Gaussian error distribution]], the [[D4:Log-Normal error distribution]] and the [[D5:Gamma error distribution]], each with its parameters restricted so that $E(1+\epsilon_t) = 1$. The truncated Normal distribution is set aside because its statistics are too complicated to summarise. The table compares their main statistics. [members D7, D6, D4, D5]

D12 [method] ETS(A,Ad,A): Pure additive exponential smoothing model with additive error, damped additive trend, and additive seasonality under Normal errors. [source B3]
D10 [method] Akram's log-ETS model: Additive error ETS model $ETS(A,Ad,A)$ with Normal errors applied to the natural logarithms of strictly positive time series and exponentiated back to the original scale. [source B4]
D9 [variable] AICc: Corrected Akaike Information Criterion used to select among ETS component structures and error distributions. [unit 1] [source B6]
D13 [method] ETS(M,Md,N): Pure multiplicative exponential smoothing model with damped multiplicative trend and no seasonality. [a kind of D1] [source B3]
D8 [variable] percentage difference (PD) ($PD$): Relative deviation of the conventional point forecast $\hat{y}_{t+h}$ from the conditional expectation $\mu_{y,t+h}$, defined as $$PD = \frac{\mu_{y,t+h} - \hat{y}_{t+h}}{\mu_{y,t+h}}$$ lying in $[0, 1)$. [unit 1]
D14 [data] Simulation experiment 1 synthetic series: 10,000 iterations of 100-observation time series simulated from $ETS(M,Md,N)$ with Inverse Gaussian errors across parameter grids $\sigma \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5, 1\}$, $\alpha, \beta \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.5\}$, $\phi \in \{0.5, 0.9, 0.95, 1\}$, and horizons $h \in \{10, 50, 100\}$.
D19 [design] Box-Cox odds regression procedure: Transformation of percentage difference $PD$ into odds $\frac{PD}{1-PD}$ followed by Box-Cox transformation ($\lambda = 0.146$) and linear regression on scaled parameters $\sigma$, horizon, $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\phi$ using `greybox::alm` (`distribution='dbcnorm'`), where $\lambda$ is the Box-Cox parameter, chosen by maximum likelihood, and each regressor, the horizon included, is rescaled to lie between 0 and 1.
D15 [data] Simulation experiment 2 synthetic series: 10,000 simulated time series of length 500 generated for each of five data-generating processes ($ETS(M,Md,M)$ with Normal, Inverse Gaussian, Gamma, Log-Normal errors, and Akram's exponentiated $ETS(A,Ad,A)$) with $\sigma=0.1$, $\alpha=0.1$, $\beta=0.05$, $\gamma=0.2$, $\phi=0.95$, $l_0=100$, $b_0=1$, and holdout horizon $48$, where $l_0$ and $b_0$ are the initial level and trend; the initial seasonal indices were generated at random and normalised.
D20 [design] 48-step quantile forecast simulation procedure: Estimation of $ETS(M,Md,M)$ with each candidate distribution, AICc-based automatic selection (`smooth::auto.adam`), and Akram's log-ETS model on 500 observations, evaluating 99 quantile levels ($\tau = 0.01, \dots, 0.99$) over a 48-step holdout.
D11 [variable] scaled pinball score (SPS) ($SPS$): Quantile forecast accuracy measure scaled by the in-sample mean $\bar{y}$, defined for probability level $\tau$ over horizon $h$ as $$SPS = \frac{1}{\bar{y}} \left( (1-\tau) \sum_{y_{t+j} < \hat{q}_{\tau,t+j}} (\hat{q}_{\tau,t+j} - y_{t+j}) + \tau \sum_{y_{t+j} \ge \hat{q}_{\tau,t+j}} (y_{t+j} - \hat{q}_{\tau,t+j}) \right).$$ Here $\hat{q}_{\tau,t+j}$ is the quantile forecast at level $\tau$ for observation $t+j$, $t$ is the last observation of the sample, and both sums run over the steps $j = 1, \dots, h$. [unit 1]
D17 [variable] quantile bias: Mean across quantile levels $\tau \in \{0.01, 0.02, \dots, 0.99\}$ of empirical quantile coverage minus nominal target level $\tau$, where the empirical coverage at level $\tau$ is the share of actual values below the quantile forecast at that level. [unit 1]
D18 [variable] quantile precision: Mean across quantile levels $\tau \in \{0.01, 0.02, \dots, 0.99\}$ of the absolute difference between empirical quantile coverage and nominal target level $\tau$. [unit 1]
D16 [data] Daily EV charging dataset: 511 daily aggregated observations of electricity used by electric vehicles in a geographical area starting from low volume with 7-day multiplicative seasonality, holding out the last 14 observations.
D21 [design] two-dimensional AICc selection procedure: Simultaneous selection of pure multiplicative ETS component structure and error distribution by minimising $AICc$ via `smooth::auto.adam` with a 14-day holdout evaluation.

ASSUMPTIONS
C12 "The multiplicative error is i.i.d. with mean one" [statistical; unchecked] The multiplicative error term $(1+\epsilon_t)$ in pure multiplicative ETS models is independent and identically distributed with unit expectation $E(1+\epsilon_t) = 1$.
C13 "The smoothing parameters of the other models are taken to lie between 0 and 1" [methodological; unchecked] The smoothing parameters $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ of the pure multiplicative ETS models other than [[D2:ETS(M,N,N)]] lie in $[0, 1]$, by analogy with the restriction derived for ETS(M,N,N) and without a derivation for these models.

CLAIMS
== 1. Properties of multiplicative error ETS models
C1 "The conventional point forecast lies below the conditional expectations" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E1, cited B3, at pp. 21-23; assumes C12, C13; limitations L1; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  In general, the conventional multi-step point forecast $\hat{y}_{t+h}$ of a pure multiplicative [[D1:ETS(M,*,*)]] model does not coincide with the conditional arithmetic expectation $\mu_{y,t+h}$ or the conditional geometric expectation $\tilde{y}_{t+h}$, satisfying $\hat{y}_{t+h} \le \tilde{y}_{t+h} \le \mu_{y,t+h}$ when smoothing parameters are non-zero.
  in plain words: For pure multiplicative ETS models, the usual multi-step point forecast is in general neither the conditional mean nor the conditional geometric mean. It is at or below the geometric mean, which is at or below the mean.
  scope: conditions: pure multiplicative ETS models with non-zero smoothing parameters and forecast horizon $h > 1$
  extends isbn 978-3-540-71918-2, at Chapter 2, pp. 21-23: Derives the explicit ordering between point forecasts, geometric conditional expectations, and arithmetic conditional expectations using logarithmic state space recursion.
C2 "When the point forecast equals the conditional expectations" [formal; supporting; extension; support: formal_proof E1; assumes C12; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  The conventional point forecast of a pure multiplicative [[D1:ETS(M,*,*)]] model coincides with both the conditional geometric and conditional arithmetic expectations only when $h=1$, when $h \le m$ for non-trended models with multiplicative seasonality, or for [[D2:ETS(M,N,N)]].
  in plain words: The point forecast equals both conditional expectations in three cases only: one step ahead; within the first seasonal cycle for seasonal models without trend; and for the level-only model, ETS(M,N,N).
  scope: conditions: pure multiplicative ETS models with unit-mean i.i.d. multiplicative errors
  extends isbn 978-3-540-71918-2, at Chapter 2, pp. 21-23: Identifies the exact boundary cases where conventional point forecasts coincide with conditional expectations.
C16 "On one M3 series the gap grows exponentially with the horizon" [descriptive; secondary; support: empirical_quant E7 (E7.R1); assumes C12; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  On [[D22:M3 series N2703]], for [[D23:ETS(M,M,N)]] the difference between the conventional point forecast and the conditional expectation obtained by simulation is negligible at short horizons and grows exponentially with the horizon from 1 to 18 steps ahead.
  in plain words: On one M3 series, the gap between the usual point forecast and the simulated conditional mean of a multiplicative trend model is negligible at short horizons and grows exponentially up to 18 steps ahead.
  scope: conditions: one series, horizons 1 to 18
  contradicts isbn 978-3-540-71918-2, at p. 23: [[B3:Hyndman et al. (2008)]] say the difference between the point forecast and the expectation will in general be negligible.
  supports C1: An example of the gap between the conventional point forecast and the conditional expectation.
== 2. Distributional assumptions in ETS framework
C5 "Closed-form concentrated likelihoods for four error distributions" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E3; assumes C12; uses C6, C7, C18; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  Concentrated log-likelihood functions for [[D1:ETS(M,*,*)]] models under the unit-mean restriction $E(1+\epsilon_t)=1$ take closed forms for [[D7:Normal error distribution]] $\mathcal{N}(1,\sigma^2)$, [[D6:Inverse Gaussian error distribution]] $\mathcal{IG}(1,\sigma^2)$, [[D4:Log-Normal error distribution]] $\log\mathcal{N}(-\frac{\sigma^2}{2},\sigma^2)$, and [[D5:Gamma error distribution]] $\Gamma(\sigma^{-2},\sigma^2)$ error distributions.
  in plain words: With the error restricted to mean one, the concentrated log-likelihood of pure multiplicative ETS has a closed form for Normal, Inverse Gaussian, Log-Normal and Gamma errors.
  scope: conditions: $ETS(M,*,*)$ models estimated via maximum likelihood on strictly positive observations
  extends isbn 978-3-540-71918-2, at p. 68: Generalises the Normal multiplicative ETS log-likelihood to Inverse Gaussian, Log-Normal, and Gamma distributions.
  supports claimset entrylib:2026.000003@v4#C17, own earlier work: Establishes the full-sample concentrated log-likelihoods for multiplicative ETS distributions later generalised to intermittent/missing-data settings in entrylib:2026.000003.
C4 "Positive error distributions apply directly to the multiplicative error" [methodological; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E3, cited B4, at Section 2; assumes C12, C13; uses C8; limitations L6; strength high (author); verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  [[D4:Log-Normal error distribution]], [[D5:Gamma error distribution]], and [[D6:Inverse Gaussian error distribution]] distributions defined on $(0, \infty)$ can be applied directly to the multiplicative error term $(1+\epsilon_t)$ of conventional [[D1:ETS(M,*,*)]] models without transforming the model into [[D10:Akram's log-ETS model]], provided $E(1+\epsilon_t)=1$.
  in plain words: Log-Normal, Gamma and Inverse Gaussian distributions can be used directly for the multiplicative error of conventional ETS models, provided the error has mean one. The model need not be rewritten in logarithms.
  scope: conditions: strictly positive time series modelled within the original $ETS(M,*,*)$ taxonomy
  extends doi 10.1111/j.1467-842X.2009.00555.x, at Section 2: Implements Log-Normal, Gamma, and Inverse Gaussian distributions directly within original $ETS(M,*,*)$ rather than sidestepping the taxonomy via logarithmic ETS.
  supports claimset entrylib:2026.000003@v4#C17, own earlier work: Provides the conventional multiplicative ETS formulation with Gamma, Log-Normal, and Inverse Gaussian errors subsequently extended to unobserved periods in entrylib:2026.000003.
C6 "Closed-form scale estimators for Normal and Inverse Gaussian errors" [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; assumes C12; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  The maximum likelihood estimator of the scale parameter $\sigma^2$ in [[D1:ETS(M,*,*)]] has the closed form $\hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \epsilon_t^2$ for the [[D7:Normal error distribution]] and $\hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \frac{\epsilon_t^2}{1+\epsilon_t}$ for the [[D6:Inverse Gaussian error distribution]].
  in plain words: With the error restricted to mean one, the maximum likelihood estimator of the scale has a closed form for Normal and for Inverse Gaussian errors.
  scope: conditions: sample size $T$ with unit-mean multiplicative error distributions
C7 "The Log-Normal scale estimator is the corrected formula" [formal; supporting; refutation; support: formal_proof E3, cited B2, own earlier work; assumes C12; limitations L3; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  Under the unit-mean restriction $\mu = -\frac{\sigma^2}{2}$, the concentrated log-likelihood of [[D1:ETS(M,*,*)]] with a [[D4:Log-Normal error distribution]] is non-convex and its maximum likelihood scale estimator is given by the corrected formula in `entrylib:2026.000003@v4#C7` rather than the quadratic root formula printed in Section 4.1 of the 2023 published article.
  in plain words: With Log-Normal errors of mean one, the concentrated log-likelihood is not convex. Its maximum likelihood scale is the corrected formula of an earlier EntryLib claim, not the root formula printed in the 2023 article.
  scope: conditions: $ETS(M,*,*)$ models with Log-Normal error distribution constrained to $E(1 + \epsilon_t) = 1$
  contradicts doi 10.1093/imaman/dpad028, at Section 4.1, pp. 9-10, own earlier work: Author erratum: the quadratic root expression for the Log-Normal scale parameter in Section 4.1 of the 2023 article was superseded by the corrected derivation in entrylib:2026.000003@v4#C7.
  extends claimset entrylib:2026.000003@v4#C7, own earlier work: References the corrected Log-Normal scale MLE formula.
C18 "No closed-form scale estimator for Gamma errors" [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E3; assumes C12; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  Under the unit-mean restriction $\xi = \sigma^{-2}$, the maximum likelihood estimator of the scale parameter $\sigma^2$ in [[D1:ETS(M,*,*)]] with the [[D5:Gamma error distribution]] has no closed form, and the method of moments gives $\hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \epsilon_t^2$.
  in plain words: With Gamma errors of mean one, the maximum likelihood estimator of the scale has no closed form; the method of moments gives the mean of the squared errors.
  scope: conditions: sample size $T$ with unit-mean multiplicative error distributions
C15 "The Inverse Gaussian is the most convenient positive distribution" [interpretive; secondary; support: argument W1, cited B8; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  Of the [[D4:Log-Normal error distribution]], the [[D5:Gamma error distribution]] and the [[D6:Inverse Gaussian error distribution]], the Inverse Gaussian is the most convenient for the error term of [[D1:ETS(M,*,*)]] models.
  in plain words: Of the three positive error distributions, the Inverse Gaussian is the easiest to use: its mean-one restriction is simple and its scale has a closed-form estimate. The argument is one of convenience, not tested.
C14 "The level-only model needs a smoothing parameter of at most one" [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E4; assumes C12; verification unchecked; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  In [[D2:ETS(M,N,N)]] with a non-negative smoothing parameter $\alpha$, the level update term $1 + \alpha\epsilon_t$ is positive for every error with $1 + \epsilon_t > 0$ only when $\alpha \le 1$.
  in plain words: In the level-only multiplicative model, the level stays positive for every positive error only when the smoothing parameter is at most one.
C8 "Only ETS(M,N,N) has a closed-form conditional mean and variance" [formal; supporting; extension; support: formal_proof E4; assumes C12; uses C14; verification agent_checked; verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  Among pure multiplicative [[D1:ETS(M,*,*)]] models, [[D2:ETS(M,N,N)]] is the only model with closed-form conditional $h$-steps-ahead mean $E(l_{t+h}|t)=l_t$ and variance $V(l_{t+h}|t)=l_t^2((1+\alpha^2\sigma^2)^h-1)$ across [[D7:Normal error distribution]], [[D6:Inverse Gaussian error distribution]], [[D4:Log-Normal error distribution]], and [[D5:Gamma error distribution]], requiring $\alpha \in [0,1]$ to ensure positivity of the level.
  in plain words: Among pure multiplicative ETS models, only the level-only model has a closed-form mean and variance h steps ahead, for all four error distributions. The level stays positive when $\alpha$ lies between 0 and 1.
  scope: conditions: $ETS(M,N,N)$ with $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ and unit-mean error distributions
  extends isbn 978-3-540-71918-2, at Chapter 6: Extends the closed-form ETS(M,N,N) conditional mean and variance formulas from Normal errors to Inverse Gaussian, Log-Normal, and Gamma distributions.
== 3. Simulation experiment 1: bias of point forecasts

  in plain words: In simulation of the damped multiplicative trend model with Inverse Gaussian errors, the gap between the conditional expectation and the point forecast grows with five parameters. The damping parameter has the largest effect and the level smoothing parameter the smallest.

  contradicts isbn 978-3-540-71918-2, at Chapter 2, p. 23: Shows that the difference between point forecasts and conditional expectations is non-negligible and grows substantially with horizon and parameter values.

WARRANTS
W1 [deductive; premises C4, C6, C7, C18] The Inverse Gaussian meets the unit-mean condition with its location set to 1, its scale has a closed-form maximum likelihood estimate, and the convolution of i.i.d. Inverse Gaussian variables is well defined. The Log-Normal has a non-convex likelihood and a scale estimate that exists only in some cases, and the Gamma has no closed-form maximum likelihood estimate of its scale. Since [[B8:Folks & Chhikara (1978)]] found that data can be described equally well by the Log-Normal, Gamma and Inverse Gaussian distributions, the choice between them can rest on convenience. The argument is not tested: in the simulations the models with the three distributions perform very similarly.

LIMITATIONS
L1 [model_specification; affects C1; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] Conditional expectations for pure multiplicative ETS models other than $ETS(M,N,N)$ lack closed-form analytical expressions and require Monte Carlo simulation.
L2 [model_specification; affects C3; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] Simulation experiment 1 evaluates point forecast bias exclusively on $ETS(M,Md,N)$ with Inverse Gaussian errors and known true parameters.
L3 [model_specification; affects C7; reduces_precision; mitigation none] The Log-Normal concentrated log-likelihood under unit-mean restriction is non-convex, creating potential local optima during numerical optimisation.
L4 [model_specification; affects C9, C10, C17; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] Simulation experiment 2 uses a single fixed parameter vector with error standard deviation $\sigma = 0.1$, where multiplicative errors remain small enough that Normal and logarithmic approximations behave regularly.
L5 [sampling; affects C11; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The real-life demonstration relies on a single proprietary daily electric vehicle charging series that cannot be publicly shared under a non-disclosure agreement.
L6 [model_specification; affects C4; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] Multi-step conditional predictive distributions for $ETS(M,*,*)$ models with Log-Normal, Gamma, or Inverse Gaussian errors have no closed form because none of these distributions is closed under both convolution and product, requiring simulation.
