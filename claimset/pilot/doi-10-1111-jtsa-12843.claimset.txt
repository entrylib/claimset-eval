CLAIMSET Online Detection of Forecast Model Inadequacies Using Forecast Errors
summary Forecasting models go wrong when the process they forecast changes, and the more complex the model, the harder the change is to spot directly. This paper proposes watching the model's one-step-ahead forecast errors instead, with a sequential changepoint test (Page's CUSUM), whatever the forecasting method. It shows that, when the model keeps a constant bias and cannot fully adapt, a change in the mean or variance of the data appears as a change in the mean of the errors or of their squares, with known limit distributions for the test. Exponential smoothing adapts to mean changes, so with it only variance changes stay visible. In simulations of seasonal data, monitoring forecast errors found changes sooner than monitoring the raw data, with few false alarms. Two examples follow: Royal Mail parcel forecasts and hospital admissions for gallstones.

DEFINITIONS
D1 [method] forecast error monitoring framework: A framework that monitors a forecasting model online by applying sequential changepoint detection to its one-step-ahead forecast errors. For a univariate series $Y_t$ and a forecasting model $\mathcal{F}$ with one-step-ahead point forecast $\hat{y}_t(1) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}}[Y_t | y_{t-1}, \ldots, y_1]$, the forecast errors are $$e_t = Y_t - \hat{y}_t(1).$$ The first $m$ forecast errors are the Phase I (training) sample; monitoring starts at $t = m + 1$ and stops when a detector, [[D2:the mean detector]] or [[D3:the squared-error detector]], crosses its threshold. Any forecasting approach can supply the errors: model-based, machine learning or expert judgement.
D2 [method] mean detector $D_\mu(m,k)$: [[D24:Page's CUSUM detector]] applied to the forecast errors of the [[D1:forecast error monitoring framework]]: $$\begin{aligned} Q_\mu(m,k) &= \sum_{t=m+1}^{m+k} e_t - \frac{k}{m} \sum_{t=1}^{m} e_t \\ D_\mu(m,k) &= \max_{0 \le i \le k} \left| Q_\mu(m,k) - Q_\mu(m,i) \right| \end{aligned}$$ with stopping time $$\tau_{\mu,m} = \min\{k \ge 1 : D_\mu(m,k) \ge \hat{\sigma}_{\mu,m} c_{\mu,\alpha} g(m,k,\gamma)\},$$ where $\hat{\sigma}_{\mu,m}$ is the standard deviation of the forecast errors in the Phase I sample. The weight function $g(m,k,\gamma)$ is that of [[D24:Page's CUSUM detector]], and the critical constant $c_{\mu,\alpha}$ comes from the limit distribution of the detector under the null hypothesis. [part of D1] [source B11]
D3 [method] squared-error detector $D_\xi(m,k)$: [[D24:Page's CUSUM detector]] applied to the centred squared forecast errors of the [[D1:forecast error monitoring framework]]: $$\begin{aligned} Q_\xi(m,k) &= \sum_{t=m+1}^{m+k} (e_t - \hat{b}_{\mu,m})^2 - \frac{k}{m} \sum_{t=1}^{m} (e_t - \hat{b}_{\mu,m})^2 \\ D_\xi(m,k) &= \max_{0 \le i \le k} \left| Q_\xi(m,k) - Q_\xi(m,i) \right| \end{aligned}$$ with stopping time $$\tau_{\xi,m} = \min\{k \ge 1 : D_\xi(m,k) \ge \hat{\sigma}_{\xi,m} c_{\xi,\alpha} g(m,k,\gamma)\},$$ where $\hat{b}_{\mu,m}$ is the mean of the forecast errors in the $m$ Phase I samples and $\hat{\sigma}_{\xi,m}$ the standard deviation of the centred squared forecast errors in that sample. $g(m,k,\gamma)$ is that of [[D24:Page's CUSUM detector]] and $c_{\xi,\alpha}$ comes from the limit distribution of the detector under the null hypothesis. The paper prints the second sum of $Q_\xi(m,k)$ without the square, a typographical error that the guarantor, an author of the paper, has confirmed. [part of D1]
  corrected from the published paper (misprint; Section 2.2, the definition of Q_ξ(m,k)): printed Q_ξ(m,k) = Σ_{t=m+1}^{m+k} (e_t − b̂_{μ,m})² − (k/m) Σ_{t=1}^{m} (e_t − b̂_{μ,m})
D4 [method] Raw CUSUM: [[D24:Page's CUSUM detector]] applied to the raw data instead of forecast errors; for variance changes, to the centred squared raw data. In the paper's simulations its standard deviation $\hat{\sigma}_m$ is the Bartlett long run variance estimator from the sandwich R package. [source B2]
D5 [method] ARMA Forecast Errors: The detector of the [[D1:forecast error monitoring framework]], [[D2:$D_\mu(m,k)$]] or [[D3:$D_\xi(m,k)$]] by the type of change, applied to the forecast errors of an ARMA forecasting model fitted with the forecast R package. For seasonal data the model is a seasonal ARMA model chosen by auto.arima using AIC, with no differencing. The standard deviation is the traditional estimator, the errors being taken as i.i.d. [a kind of D1] [source B12]
D6 [method] ETS Forecast Errors: The detector of the [[D1:forecast error monitoring framework]], [[D2:$D_\mu(m,k)$]] or [[D3:$D_\xi(m,k)$]] by the type of change, applied to the forecast errors of an ETS forecasting model fitted with the es function of the smooth R package. The standard deviation is the traditional estimator, the errors being taken as i.i.d. [a kind of D1] [source B13]
D7 [method] compared detectors: The three detectors compared in the paper's simulations. [members D4, D5, D6]
D8 [data] mean change model: A data-generating process with a changepoint at an unknown location $k^*$: $$\begin{aligned} Y_t | y_{t-1}, \ldots, y_1 &\sim F, \quad t = 1, \ldots, m + k^* \\ Y_t | y_{t-1}, \ldots, y_1 &\sim G, \quad t = m + k^* + 1, m + k^* + 2, \ldots \end{aligned}$$ where $F$ and $G$ differ in expectation by $\delta_{\mu,m} \ne 0$. The raw data may be temporally dependent, trended, seasonal or depend on exogenous variables.
D9 [data] mean and variance change model: The [[D8:mean change model]] extended so that the pre-change distribution $F$ and the post-change distribution $G'$ differ in expectation by $\delta_{\mu,m}$ and in variance by $\delta_{\xi,m}$ (the paper's Definition 1). [a kind of D8]
D10 [method] ETS(A,N,N) forecasting model: The local level exponential smoothing model with additive error, no trend and no seasonality, whose one-step-ahead forecasts follow $$\hat{y}_t(1) = \hat{y}_{t-1}(1) + \alpha e_{t-1},$$ where $e_{t-1}$ is the forecast error at time $t-1$ and $0 < \alpha < 1$ is the smoothing parameter.
D11 [method] ARMA forecasting model: A forecasting model for the causal and invertible ARMA($p$,$q$) process $$\phi(B)(Y_t - \lambda_t) = \theta(B)\epsilon_t,$$ with mean parameters $\lambda_t$, autoregressive polynomial $\phi(B) = 1 - \phi_1 B - \cdots - \phi_p B^p$, moving average polynomial $\theta(B) = 1 + \theta_1 B + \cdots + \theta_q B^q$ and independent zero-mean errors $\epsilon_t$ with variance $\sigma_t^2$.
D12 [variable] average detection delay (ADD): The mean of the detection delays over the simulated series in which a change was signalled within the length of the data.
D13 [variable] detection probability (DP): The proportion of simulated series in which a change was detected within the length of the monitoring data.
D14 [variable] false detection probability (FDP): The proportion of simulated series in which a change was detected before the true change had occurred.
D15 [design] simulation design of the paper: Each scenario is simulated 1000 times with normally distributed innovations. Forecasting models are trained on the first $n_{\text{train}} = 300$ observations and then produce one-step-ahead forecasts sequentially. A change is signalled by the theoretical thresholds of [[D24:Page's CUSUM detector]] with $\gamma = 0$ and a false alarm rate of 0.05.
D16 [data] seasonal mean change scenario: Simulated data whose errors follow an ARMA(2,1) model with $\phi = (-0.6, 0.3)$, $\theta = -0.3$, $\lambda = 0$ and $\sigma^2 = 1$, with a mean change of size $\delta_\mu$ at $k^* = 100$ and a seasonal component of frequency 12 whose seasonal means are 12 equally spaced points of $y = 10\sin(x)$, $x \in [0, \pi]$.
D17 [data] seasonal trend change scenario: Simulated data $y_t = 1[t > m + k^*]\beta t + \epsilon_t$ whose errors follow an ARMA(1,1) model with $\phi = 0.2$, $\theta = 0.2$, $\lambda = 0$ and $\sigma^2 = 1$: no trend before the change and a trend of gradient $\beta$ after it, with a seasonal component of frequency 7 whose seasonal means are 7 equally spaced points of $y = 10\cos(x)$, $x \in [0, 2\pi]$.
D18 [data] autoregressive parameter change scenarios: Simulated data with AR(1) errors, $\lambda = 0$, whose autoregressive parameter changes at the changepoint from $\phi_{\text{pre}} = 0.2$ (scenario 1) or $\phi_{\text{pre}} = 0.8$ (scenario 2) to varying values $\phi_{\text{post}}$, with quarterly seasonality given by quarter means $(-2, 5, 7, -10)$. The ETS forecasting model is ETS(A,N,A) with estimated smoothing parameters.
D19 [data] Royal Mail forecast errors: Forecast errors of the daily number of parcels to be delivered from one Royal Mail delivery office, from 1 July 2020 to 31 December 2020. The underlying data and forecasts are not known to the authors. July to October is the Phase I sample; monitoring starts on 1 November 2020.
D20 [data] GRP admissions series: The monthly proportion of A&E admissions related to gallstone-related pathology (GRP) in participating hospitals across England, 2010 to 2020, based on NHS Hospital Episode Statistics. A SARMA(1,0,0)(1,0,0)$_{12}$ model fitted to the data up to 2013 gives one-step-ahead forecasts for the rest; 2013 to 2016 is the Phase I sample of size $m = 36$ and monitoring starts in 2016. [source B4]
D21 [method] model-based sequential changepoint detection: Sequential changepoint detection that fits the model to the data before and after a potential changepoint and decides by a likelihood-ratio style statistic.
D22 [variable] detection delay: The number of observations between a change and the time a detector signals it.
D23 [variable] date of detection: The date of the observation at which a detector first crosses its threshold.
D24 [method] Page's CUSUM detector $D(m,k)$: The sequential detector of a mean change in a series $Y_t$ that follows the change in mean model $$Y_t = \begin{cases} \lambda + \epsilon_t, & t = 1, \ldots, m + k^* \\ \lambda + \epsilon_t + \Delta_m, & t = m + k^* + 1, m + k^* + 2, \ldots \end{cases}$$ with zero-mean variables $\epsilon_t$, a training period of length $m$ without changepoints and a change of size $\Delta_m$ at time $k^*$ after it. With $$\begin{aligned} Q(m,k) &= \sum_{t=m+1}^{m+k} Y_t - \frac{k}{m} \sum_{t=1}^{m} Y_t \\ D(m,k) &= \max_{0 \le i \le k} \left| Q(m,k) - Q(m,i) \right| \end{aligned}$$ the null hypothesis $H_0: \Delta_m = 0$ is rejected in favour of $H_A: \Delta_m \ne 0$ at the stopping time $$\tau_m = \min\{k \ge 1 : D(m,k) \ge \hat{\sigma}_m c_\alpha g(m,k,\gamma)\},$$ where $\hat{\sigma}_m$ is a weakly consistent estimator, from the $m$ training samples, of the constant $\sigma$ of the weak invariance principle, the weight function is $$g(m,k,\gamma) = \sqrt{m}\left(1 + \frac{k}{m}\right)\left(\frac{k}{m+k}\right)^{\gamma}, \quad \gamma \in [0, 1/2).$$ and the critical value $c_\alpha$ is taken from the limit distribution under $H_0$, so that $\lim_{m\to\infty} P(\tau_m < \infty) = \alpha$ under $H_0$ for the chosen false alarm rate $\alpha$ and $\lim_{m\to\infty} P(\tau_m < \infty) = 1$ under $H_A$. All limits are for $m \to \infty$; the model is trained once and not re-trained during monitoring. The paper takes $\gamma = 0$. [source B10]
D25 [data] supplementary mean change scenarios: Simulated series of length 1000 from (1) an AR(2) process with $\phi = (0.5, -0.3)$ and (2) an ARMA(1,1) process with $\phi = 0.6$ and $\theta = 0.3$, both with $\lambda = 0$ and $\sigma^2 = 1$ (arima.sim, 50 burn-in points removed), with a mean change of size $\delta_\mu$ at $k^* = 100$ after monitoring begins; the forecasting models are trained on $n_{\text{train}} = 200$ observations, the detectors use $m = 200$, and 500 time points remain to detect the change. The ARMA forecasting model has the known order with parameters estimated in the training period; the Raw CUSUM does not use the first 200 points.
D26 [data] supplementary variance change scenarios: Simulated series as in the [[D25:supplementary mean change scenarios]] ($n = 1000$, $n_{\text{train}} = 200$, $m = 200$, $k^* = 100$) from (1) an AR(1) process with $\phi = 0.9$ and (2) an ARMA(2,1) process with $\phi = (0.5, -0.3)$ and $\theta = 0.6$, both with $\lambda = 0$ and $\sigma^2 = 1$, whose variance changes to $\delta_\xi$ at the changepoint. The ARMA forecasting model has the known order with estimated parameters; the ETS forecasting model is ETS(A,N,N) with an estimated smoothing parameter. The Supporting Information's Figure 2 labels the second scenario ARMA(1,2).

CLAIMS
C1 "Mean changes appear in the forecast errors" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E1; assumes C18, C19; limitations L1; verification agent_checked]
  Under [[D8:the mean change model]], the one-step-ahead forecast errors of a forecasting model that satisfies the two forecasting-model assumptions have a change in mean of size $\Delta_{\mu,m} \ne 0$ at the changepoint: $e_t = b_\mu + \epsilon_{\mu,t}$ for $t \le m + k^*$ and $e_t = b_\mu + \epsilon_{\mu,t} + \Delta_{\mu,m}$ afterwards.
  scope: data_type: univariate time series | conditions: one-step-ahead forecasts; a single changepoint after the training sample
  extends B1: Aue et al. (2015) monitor the residuals of ARMA models only; the claim covers the forecast errors of any forecasting model that satisfies the two assumptions.
C2 "Variance and mean changes appear in the squared forecast errors" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E2; assumes C18, C19; limitations L8; verification agent_checked]
  Under [[D9:the mean and variance change model]] and the constant-bias assumption, the change in the mean of the centred squared forecast errors is $$\Delta_{\xi,m} = \mathrm{Var}_{G'}[Y_t|\tilde{y}_t] - \mathrm{Var}_F[Y_t|\tilde{y}_t]$$ when $\delta_{\mu,m} = 0$, and $$\Delta_{\xi,m} = \mathrm{Var}_{G'}[Y_t|\tilde{y}_t] - \mathrm{Var}_F[Y_t|\tilde{y}_t] + \Delta_{\mu,m}^2$$ when $\delta_{\mu,m} \ne 0$ and the incomplete-adjustment assumption holds.
  scope: data_type: univariate time series | conditions: one-step-ahead forecasts
  extends B1: Aue et al. (2015) monitor the residuals of ARMA models only; the claim covers the forecast errors of any forecasting model that satisfies the two assumptions.
C3 "Limit distributions of the squared-error detector" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E3; assumes C18, C19, C20, C21; uses C2, C24, C25; limitations L6, L7; verification disputed]
  For the [[D3:squared-error detector]] under [[D9:the mean and variance change model]], the limit distribution of Theorem 1 of the paper's Supporting Information holds under the null hypothesis of no change, and the limit distribution of its Theorem 2 holds for the stopping time $\tau_{\xi,m}$ under the alternative when the change size $\Delta_{\xi,m}$ is large enough in the sense of that document's Assumptions C to E.
  scope: conditions: change sizes satisfying Assumptions C to E of the paper's Supporting Information
  extends B1: Aue et al. (2015) monitor the residuals of ARMA models only; the claim covers the forecast errors of any forecasting model that satisfies the two assumptions.
C4 "ETS(A,N,N) forecast errors return to zero after a mean change" [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E4; verification agent_checked]
  For an [[D10:ETS(A,N,N) forecasting model]] under [[D8:the mean change model]], with forecasts unbiased before the changepoint and a conditional mean of the data equal to $\lambda$ before it and $\lambda + \delta_{\mu,m}$ after it, the expected forecast error $s$ steps after the changepoint is $\mathbb{E}[e_{m+k^*+s}] = \delta_{\mu,m}(1-\alpha)^{s-1}$ for $s = 1, 2, \ldots$, an expectation that returns to zero as $s$ grows when $0 < \alpha < 1$, so the model does not satisfy the incomplete-adjustment assumption.
  scope: conditions: ETS(A,N,N); a change in mean
  addresses B1: The closest result found: Aue et al. (2015) give the change size in ARMA residuals; the claim gives it for an exponential smoothing forecasting model, where it vanishes.
  corrected from the published paper (misprint; Proposition 3, Section 3.2): printed E[e_{k*+s}] = (λ + δ_{μ,m})(1 − Σ_{i=0}^{s−2} α(1−α)^i) − λ(α(1−α)^{s−1} − (1−α)^s) for s = 2, 3, …

C18 [formal; supporting; assumption (theorem_condition, unchecked)]
  The forecasting model has a constant bias before the changepoint: $\mathbb{E}_F[Y_t|\tilde{y}_t] - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}}[Y_t|\tilde{y}_t] = b_\mu$ for $t = 1, \ldots, m + k^*$, where $b_\mu$ is unknown and may be zero (the paper's Assumption 1, the constant-bias assumption).
C19 [formal; supporting; assumption (theorem_condition, unchecked)]
  After the changepoint the forecasting model does not fully adjust to the change: $$\mathbb{E}_G[Y_t|\tilde{y}_t] - \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}}[Y_t|\tilde{y}_t] = b_\mu + \Delta_{\mu,m}$$ for $t = m + k^* + 1, m + k^* + 2, \ldots$, where $\Delta_{\mu,m} \ne 0$ is a function of the change size in the raw data (the paper's Assumption 2, the incomplete-adjustment assumption).
C20 [formal; supporting; assumption (statistical, unchecked)]
  The zero-mean variables $\epsilon_t$ of the monitored series, here $\epsilon_{\mu,t}$ and $\epsilon_{\xi,t}$ left in the forecast errors after the bias is removed, satisfy the weak invariance principle of Assumptions A and B of the paper's Supporting Information: $\left|\sum_{t=1}^{m} \epsilon_t\right| = O_P(\sqrt{m})$, and there are Wiener processes $\{W_m(i) : i \ge 0, m \ge 1\}$ and a constant $\sigma > 0$ with $$\sup_{1/m \le i < \infty} \frac{1}{(mi)^{1/\nu}} \left| \sum_{t=m+1}^{m+mi} \epsilon_t - \sigma W_m(mi) \right| = O_P(1) \quad \text{for some } \nu > 2$$, and are independent and identically distributed, so that the ordinary standard deviation of the Phase I errors estimates $\sigma$.
C21 [formal; supporting; assumption (theorem_condition, unchecked)]
  The change is large enough and bounded: the changepoint is $k^* = \lfloor \theta m^\beta \rfloor$ for some $\theta > 0$ and $0 \le \beta < 1$, $\sqrt{m}\,|\Delta_m| \to \infty$ as $m \to \infty$, and $\Delta_m = O(1)$, where $\Delta_m$ is the change size in the monitored series, here $\Delta_{\mu,m}$ or $\Delta_{\xi,m}$, as Assumptions C to E of the paper's Supporting Information require.
C24 "Null limit distribution of Page's CUSUM detector" [formal; supporting; synthesis; support: cited B8; assumes C20]
  For [[D24:Page's CUSUM detector]] under the null hypothesis of no change, $$\lim_{m\to\infty} P\left(\frac{1}{\hat{\sigma}_m} \sup_{1 \le k < \infty} \frac{D(m,k)}{g(m,k,\gamma)} \le c\right) = P\left(\sup_{0<i<1} \sup_{0 \le j \le i} \frac{1}{i^\gamma} \left| W(i) - \frac{1-i}{1-j} W(j) \right| \le c\right)$$ for every real $c$, where $\{W(i) : i \in [0,1]\}$ is a standard Brownian motion (Theorem 1 of the paper's Supporting Information, from Fremdt 2015).
  scope: conditions: no change in the monitored series; limit for a training sample size going to infinity
C25 "Limit distribution of the stopping time under a mean change" [formal; supporting; synthesis; support: cited B9; assumes C20, C21]
  For [[D24:Page's CUSUM detector]] under the alternative of a mean change, $$\lim_{m\to\infty} P\left(\frac{\tau_m - a_m(c_\alpha)}{b_m(c_\alpha)} \le x\right) = \Psi(x)$$ for every real $x$, where $a_m(c)$ is the unique solution of $$a_m(c) = \left(\frac{\sigma c\, m^{1/2-\gamma}}{|\Delta_m|} + \frac{k^*}{(a_m(c))^\gamma}\right)^{1/(1-\gamma)},$$ $$b_m(c) = \frac{\sigma \sqrt{a_m(c)}}{|\Delta_m|} \left(1 - \gamma\left(1 - \frac{k^*}{a_m(c)}\right)\right)^{-1},$$ and $\Psi(x) = 1 - \tilde{\Psi}(-x)$ with $\tilde{\Psi}(x)$ equal to $\Phi(x)$ when $\tilde{\Delta}_m = |\Delta_m| m^{\beta(1-\gamma)-1/2+\gamma} \to 0$, to $P(\sup_{d<i<1} W(i) \le x)$ when $\tilde{\Delta}_m \to \tilde{C} \in (0,\infty)$, and to $P(\sup_{0<i<1} W(i) \le x)$ when $\tilde{\Delta}_m \to \infty$, where $\Phi$ is the standard normal distribution function, $W$ a standard Brownian motion and $d = d(c)$ the unique solution of $d = 1 - \frac{c}{C} d^{1-\gamma}$ with $C = \theta^{1-\gamma}\tilde{C}$ (Theorem 2 of the paper's Supporting Information, from Fremdt 2014).

WARRANTS
W1 [deductive; premises C2, C3, C4] The variance-change formula of Proposition 1 needs only the constant-bias assumption, which an ETS forecasting model can satisfy, so a variance change in the raw data gives a mean change in its squared forecast errors; the limit distributions of the squared-error detector then apply. The mean of the forecast errors themselves returns to zero after a mean change, so only the variance change remains detectable.
W2 [deductive; premises C13, C14] A model-based sequential detector must fit the post-change model, here a SARMA(1,0,0)(1,0,0) model with a 12-month season, which the paper states needs at least two years of data after the change; the detectors on the forecast errors signalled four and two observations after it.
W3 [abductive; premises C10] The Raw CUSUM monitors the raw data, whose quarterly seasonal pattern (quarter means -2, 5, 7 and -10) it does not model, so its estimate of the variance in the Phase I (training) period includes the seasonal variation and is too large. The threshold grows with that estimate, so a change must be larger to be detected. The forecasting models capture the seasonality, and their forecast errors do not carry it. The paper gives this as the reason for the results of the autoregressive parameter change scenarios; it does not report the variance estimates.

LIMITATIONS
L1 [model_specification; affects C1; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] Forecasting models that adapt to a change in mean, such as ETS models with a positive smoothing parameter, do not satisfy the incomplete-adjustment assumption, so a mean change does not stay in their forecast errors.
L2 [model_specification; affects C5, C8, C9, C10, C11, C12; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] The two forecasting-model assumptions that make the thresholds theoretically valid are not necessarily satisfied in all the simulated scenarios.
L3 [sampling; affects C5, C8, C9, C10, C27, C28, C29; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] All simulated data have an ARMA error structure with normally distributed innovations and a training sample of 300 observations (200 in the simulations of the Supporting Information).
L4 [measurement; affects C6; reduces_precision; mitigation none] The start of the Christmas peak season at the delivery office is inferred from the rise in the mean and variance of the forecast errors; the underlying parcel volumes and forecasts are not known to the authors.
L5 [sample_size; affects C13, C14, C7; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The application uses one monthly series with a Phase I sample of 36 observations.
L6 [other; affects C3, C17; reduces_precision; mitigation none] The limit distribution under a variance change (Proposition 2) is taken from Theorem 2 of the Supporting Information, which assumes one invariance constant σ for the whole series; a variance change alters the long-run variance of the centred squared forecast errors, so the theorem does not apply as stated and the paper does not address this. Found by the independent proof check.
L7 [other; affects C3, C23; reduces_precision; mitigation none] The limit distributions of the detectors need the monitored errors to keep their distribution, apart from the change in mean, after the changepoint, and the null hypothesis is that the errors do not change (Δ = 0), not that the raw data do not; the paper states neither. Found by the independent proof check.
L8 [model_specification; affects C2, C17; reduces_precision; mitigation none] When only the variance changes, the change in the squared forecast errors equals the change in variance only if the forecast bias stays b_μ after the change and the conditional variances are constant; for a forecasting model that is not the true one the shift is another multiple: ETS(A,N,N) with α = 0.5 on independent data gives 2δ_ξ/(2 − α). Found by the second proof check.

OPEN QUESTIONS
O1 [from C1, C2] Whether a multivariate sequential changepoint algorithm on the vector of forecast errors of several variables or products detects changes, and how it should treat the sparsity of a change, is left as future work.
O2 [from C1] How to combine the information in forecast errors from several forecast horizons, beyond the one-step-ahead errors studied, is not established.
