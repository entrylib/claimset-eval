CLAIMSET Multi-step estimators and shrinkage effect in time series models
summary Forecasting models are sometimes estimated by minimising errors several steps ahead rather than one step ahead, which makes forecasts more robust when the model is misspecified. Why this happens had lacked a general explanation. Using a linear state space framework covering ARIMA and exponential smoothing models, this paper shows that multi-step loss functions automatically shrink dynamic model parameters toward zero in finite samples, whereas static regression parameters experience no shrinkage. Because strong shrinkage over long horizons can introduce estimation bias, the paper derives a General Predictive Likelihood, which avoids shrinkage when the model is correctly specified. It then proposes the Geometric Trace Mean Squared Error (GTMSE) estimator to moderate shrinkage. Simulations and real-data tests on Greenland surface temperatures and airline passengers show that GTMSE improves parameter efficiency over existing multi-step estimators and outperforms one-step estimation under model misspecification.

DEFINITIONS
D1 [theory] SSOE state space model: An additive-error linear state space model of the form $$\begin{aligned} y_t &= w' v_{t-1} + \epsilon_t \\ v_t &= F v_{t-1} + g \epsilon_t \end{aligned}$$ where $y_t$ is the observed value at time $t$, $v_t$ is the state vector, $F$ is the transition matrix, $g$ is the persistence vector, $w$ is the measurement vector, and $\epsilon_t \sim \text{i.i.d. } \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_1^2)$, with multi-step coefficients $c_j = w' F^{j-1} g$. [source B1]
D2 [method] MSE_1: Conventional one-step-ahead mean squared error estimator minimising $$MSE_1 = \frac{1}{T-1} \sum_{t=1}^{T-1} e_{t+1|t}^2$$ where $e_{t+1|t} = y_{t+1} - \hat{\mu}_{t+1|t}$ is the one-step-ahead in-sample forecast error over $T$ observations.
D3 [method] MSE_h: Multi-step mean squared error estimator minimising the sample squared error at a specific forecast horizon $h$: $$MSE_h = \frac{1}{T-h} \sum_{t=1}^{T-h} e_{t+h|t}^2$$ where $e_{t+h|t} = y_{t+h} - \hat{\mu}_{t+h|t}$ is the conditional $h$-step-ahead forecast error. [source B2]
D4 [method] TMSE: Trace Mean Squared Error estimator minimising the sum of squared forecast errors across horizons $1$ to $h$: $$TMSE = \frac{1}{T-h} \sum_{j=1}^{h} \sum_{t=1}^{T-h} e_{t+j|t}^2$$ [source B3]
D5 [method] MSCE: Mean Squared Cumulative Error estimator minimising the squared sum of forecast errors over horizons $1$ to $h$: $$MSCE = \frac{1}{T-h} \sum_{t=1}^{T-h} \left( \sum_{j=1}^{h} e_{t+j|t} \right)^2$$ [source B4]
D6 [method] GTMSE: Geometric Trace Mean Squared Error estimator introduced in this paper, minimising the sum of logarithmic multi-step mean squared errors across horizons $1$ to $h$: $$GTMSE = \sum_{j=1}^{h} \log \left( \frac{1}{T-h} \sum_{t=1}^{T-h} e_{t+j|t}^2 \right)$$
D7 [method] General Predictive Likelihood: General Predictive Likelihood for consecutive observations $Y_t = (y_{t+1}, \dots, y_{t+h})'$ under Gaussian errors, whose concentrated log-likelihood is $$l_{GPL_h}(\theta, \hat{\Sigma} \mid Y) = -\frac{T-h}{2} \left( h \log(2\pi e) + \log|\hat{\Sigma}| \right)$$ where $\hat{\Sigma} = \frac{1}{T-h} \sum_{t=1}^{T-h} E_t E_t'$ is the sample multi-step forecast error covariance matrix.
D8 [method] Generalised Variance: Determinant of the sample multi-step forecast error covariance matrix, $$GV = |\hat{\Sigma}|$$ whose minimisation is equivalent to maximising the [[D7:General Predictive Likelihood]].
D9 [method] ETS(A,N,N): Pure additive local-level exponential smoothing model in the Gardner taxonomy with level smoothing parameter $\alpha$, multi-step coefficient $\hat{c}_j = \hat{\alpha}$, and $h$-step conditional variance $s_h^2 = s_1^2(1 + (h-1)\hat{\alpha}^2)$. [source B6]
D10 [method] ARIMA(0,1,1): First-order integrated moving average model $(1-B)y_t = (1+\theta B)\epsilon_t$ with moving average parameter $\theta$. [source B7]
D11 [method] ARIMA(1,1,1): First-order autoregressive integrated moving average model $(1-B)(1-\phi_1 B)y_t = (1+\theta_1 B)\epsilon_t$ with AR parameter $\phi_1$ and MA parameter $\theta_1$. [source B7]
D12 [method] ETS(A,A,N): Pure additive local-trend exponential smoothing model in the Gardner taxonomy with level smoothing parameter $\alpha$ and trend smoothing parameter $\beta$, multi-step coefficient $\hat{c}_j = \hat{\alpha} + \hat{\beta}j$, and $h$-step conditional variance $s_h^2 = s_1^2\left(1 + \sum_{j=1}^{h-1}(\hat{\alpha} + \hat{\beta}j)^2\right)$. [source B6]
D13 [data] HadCRUT4 Greenland Mean Surface Temperature series: Monthly Mean Surface Temperature time series from HadCRUT.4.5.0.0 at grid cell $(322.5^\circ, 62.5^\circ)$ in Greenland, January 1849 to December 2017 (2,016 observations total; first 1,956 for training and final 60 for holdout evaluation).
D14 [data] Air Passengers series: Classical monthly airline passenger totals time series from Box and Jenkins (1976) evaluated with a misspecified pure additive $ETS(A,A,A)$ model over a 12-month forecast horizon. [source B7]
D15 [method] compared estimators: Group of all six parameter estimators compared in the empirical evaluations: [[D2:MSE_1]], [[D3:MSE_h]], [[D4:TMSE]], [[D6:GTMSE]], [[D5:MSCE]], and [[D7:General Predictive Likelihood]]. [members D2, D3, D4, D6, D5, D7]
D16 [variable] RMSE: Root Mean Squared Error over forecast horizon $h$: $$RMSE = \sqrt{\frac{1}{h} \sum_{j=1}^{h} e_{t+j|t}^2}$$
D17 [variable] MAE: Mean Absolute Error over forecast horizon $h$: $$MAE = \frac{1}{h} \sum_{j=1}^{h} |e_{t+j|t}|$$
D18 [variable] ME: Mean Error measuring forecast bias over forecast horizon $h$: $$ME = \frac{1}{h} \sum_{j=1}^{h} e_{t+j|t}$$
D19 [method] multi-step estimators: Multi-step forecast error estimators studied in the paper: [[D3:MSE_h]], [[D4:TMSE]], [[D5:MSCE]], and [[D6:GTMSE]], minimising functions of $e_{t+j|t}^2$. [members D3, D4, D5, D6]
D20 [method] existing multi-step estimators: Existing multi-step forecast error estimators prior to GTMSE: [[D3:MSE_h]], [[D4:TMSE]], and [[D5:MSCE]], minimising functions of $e_{t+j|t}^2$. [members D3, D4, D5]
D21 [variable] parameter estimation bias and dispersion: Distribution of estimated model parameters across 500 Monte Carlo replications evaluated visually via boxplots for median bias and interquartile range.

CLAIMS
C1 "Multi-step losses shrink dynamic parameters" [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E1; assumes C19, C20; uses C8, C9, C10; limitations L1; verification unchecked; verifiability open]
  Minimising sample multi-step forecast error loss functions in [[D19:multi-step estimators]] shrinks the dynamic parameters of univariate [[D1:SSOE state space models]] toward zero in finite samples relative to [[D2:MSE_1]], with shrinkage intensity increasing with the forecast horizon $h$ and decreasing with the sample size $T$.
  scope: conditions: finite samples T > h > 1; univariate additive-error state space models | excludes: static linear regression models with persistence vector g = 0
  addresses B2, at Section 1, p. 623: Tiao and Xu (1993) observed in simulations that multi-step estimation produces more conservative models and pushes MA parameters toward boundary values on finite samples, without providing a general theoretical explanation.
C2 "No shrinkage in static regression" [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E1; assumes C19; limitations L2; verification agent_checked; verifiability open]
  Minimising multi-step forecast error loss functions in [[D19:multi-step estimators]] imposes no parameter shrinkage on static linear regression models.
  scope: conditions: static linear regression models without dynamic state transitions
  extends B10, at Section 2, p. 203: Chevillon and Hendry (2005) analysed direct multi-step estimation in dynamic autoregressions, whereas static linear regression lacks dynamic state transitions.
C3 "GPL behaves like MSE_1 under correct specification" [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E2; assumes C19, C21; uses C7, C13; limitations L3, L12; verification disputed; verifiability open]
  Maximising the [[D7:General Predictive Likelihood]] gives parameter estimates similar to those of the conventional one-step-ahead estimator [[D2:MSE_1]] under correct model specification.
  scope: conditions: correctly specified linear state space models with Gaussian errors | excludes: misspecified models where |\hat{A}| \neq 1
  extends B5, at p. 77: Clements and Hendry (1998) formulate the multi-step predictive log-likelihood and note diagonal covariance under exact assumptions.
C4 "GTMSE shrinks more mildly" [formal; principal; support: formal_proof E2; assumes C19; limitations L4; verification unchecked; verifiability open]
  The proposed [[D6:GTMSE]] estimator imposes a milder parameter shrinkage effect than [[D20:existing multi-step estimators]] in finite samples.
  scope: conditions: univariate state space models estimated on finite samples with h > 1
  addresses B3, at p. 484: Weiss and Andersen (1984) introduced the additive Trace Mean Squared Error (TMSE) multi-step loss function across horizons 1 to h.
C5 "GTMSE the least biased multi-step estimator in simulations" [descriptive; principal; support: simulation E3 artefacts A1; uses C14; limitations L5; strength moderate (author); verifiability unverifiable; planning exploratory]
  In Monte Carlo simulations of [[D9:ETS(A,N,N)]] and [[D10:ARIMA(0,1,1)]] processes, [[D6:GTMSE]] produces less biased and more efficient parameter estimates with lower [[D21:parameter estimation bias and dispersion]] than [[D20:existing multi-step estimators]].
  scope: conditions: sample sizes T \in \{20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000, 5000\} with T > h; horizons h \in \{10, 20, 50, 100, 200\} | data_type: simulated univariate time series
  addresses B2, at Section 3, pp. 628-633: Tiao and Xu (1993) demonstrated via simulation that multi-step estimators lose finite-sample parameter efficiency relative to one-step MSE as horizon increases.
C6 "GTMSE more accurate than MSE_1 on both series" [descriptive; principal; support: empirical_quant E4 (E4.T1), empirical_quant E5 (E5.T1); uses C16, C17; limitations L6; strength moderate (author); verifiability open; planning exploratory]
  On the [[D13:HadCRUT4 Greenland Mean Surface Temperature series]] and the misspecified [[D14:Air Passengers series]], [[D6:GTMSE]] achieves lower out-of-sample [[D16:RMSE]], [[D17:MAE]], and absolute [[D18:ME]] than [[D2:MSE_1]].
  scope: conditions: 60-month holdout on Greenland temperature and 12-month holdout on Air Passengers | data_type: monthly univariate time series
  extends B11, at Section 1, p. 266: Proietti (2011) showed that multi-step estimation gains over one-step estimation depend on model misspecification and data characteristics.
C7 "Forecast errors of different horizons are correlated" [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E1; assumes C19; verification agent_checked; verifiability open]
  In an [[D1:SSOE state space model]] with independent homoscedastic errors, the covariance between the $i$-step-ahead and $j$-step-ahead forecast errors, for $1 \le i < j$, is $$\sigma_{i,j} = \sigma_1^2 \left(c_{j-i} + \sum_{l=1}^{i-1} c_{i-l}\, c_{j-l}\right),$$ with an empty sum when $i = 1$, and $\sigma_{j,i} = \sigma_{i,j}$, so forecast errors of different horizons are correlated unless every $c_j$ is zero.
  contradicts B8, at p. 11: Martinez (2017) assumes zero correlation between multi-step forecast errors when assumptions hold.
  corrected from the published paper (misprint; Equation (12), Proposition 2.2): printed σ_{i,j} = σ_1² c_{|i−j|} when i = 1 or j = 1, and σ_1²(c_{|i−j|} + Σ_{l=1}^{i−1} c_{|l−j|} c_{|i−l|}) otherwise, with no order of i and j
C8 "ARIMA(1,1,1) degenerates into ARIMA(1,0,0)" [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E1; assumes C19, C20; limitations L1, L7; verification disputed; verifiability open]
  Minimising multi-step forecast error loss functions in [[D19:multi-step estimators]] for an [[D11:ARIMA(1,1,1)]] model shrinks both $\hat{\phi}_1$ and $\hat{\theta}_1$ toward $-1$ as the forecast horizon $h$ increases, degenerating the model into a restricted $ARIMA(1,0,0)$ model.
C9 "ETS models become deterministic as the horizon grows" [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E1; assumes C19, C20; limitations L1; verification unchecked; verifiability open]
  Minimising multi-step loss functions in [[D19:multi-step estimators]] shrinks smoothing parameters toward zero in pure additive exponential smoothing models, turning [[D9:ETS(A,N,N)]], [[D12:ETS(A,A,N)]], $ETS(A,A_d,N)$, and $ETS(A,N,A)$ into deterministic models as $h \to \infty$.
  extends B9: Kourentzes and Trapero (2018) examined MSE_h and TMSE parameter effects on selected ETS models.
C10 "Stepwise shrinkage in ETS(A,N,A)" [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E1; assumes C19, C20; limitations L1, L8; verification disputed; verifiability open]
  In $ETS(A,N,A)$ with periodicity $m$, minimising multi-step loss functions in [[D19:multi-step estimators]] shrinks the seasonal smoothing parameter $\hat{\gamma}$ in a stepwise manner only when $h > m$, and faster than $\hat{\alpha}$ once $h > 2m$.
  corrected from the published paper (misprint; Table 1, row ETS(A,N,A)): printed c_j = α + γ j_m with j_m = ⌊(j−1)/m⌋
C13 "Generalised Variance reduces to the one-step variance" [formal; supporting; support: formal_proof E2; assumes C19, C21; verification agent_checked; verifiability open]
  For the model-implied covariance matrix of the 1- to $h$-step forecast errors, built from $s_1^2$ and the $\hat{c}_j$ as in Appendix D, the logarithm of its [[D8:Generalised Variance]] decomposes as $\log GV = h \log s_1^2 + \log |\hat{A}|$, where $|\hat{A}| = 1$ under correct model specification.

C19 [formal; supporting; assumption (statistical, unchecked); verifiability not_applicable]

C20 [formal; supporting; assumption (statistical, unchecked); verifiability not_applicable]

E1 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and linear algebra] Analytical derivation in the single source of error (SSOE) state space framework showing that multi-step loss functions minimise multi-step forecast error variances and covariances, shrinking dynamic state-space parameters toward zero (and regression parameters not at all because $g=0$), along with specific derivations for ARIMA(1,1,1) and pure additive ETS models (Sections 2, 3, and Appendices A–B).
  S1 \eta_{t+h|t} = y_{t+h} - \mu_{t+h|t} = \sum_{j=1}^{h-1} c_{h-j}\, \epsilon_{t+j} + \epsilon_{t+h}, \quad c_j = w' F^{j-1} g (by D1; definition)
  S2 \sigma_h^2 = \sigma_1^2 \left(1 + \sum_{j=1}^{h-1} c_j^2\right), \quad \sigma_{i,j} = \sigma_1^2 \left(c_{j-i} + \sum_{l=1}^{i-1} c_{i-l}\, c_{j-l}\right) \text{ for } 1 \le i < j (by S1, C19; variance and covariance of linear combinations)
  S3 \text{Minimising } MSE_h, TMSE, \text{ or } MSCE \text{ minimises } s_1^2 \text{ and } \hat{c}_j^2 \text{ toward } 0 \text{ for } h > 1, \text{ whereas } g=0 \text{ in static regression implies } c_j = 0 \text{ and no shrinkage.} (by S2; sample substitution and minimisation)
  corrected from the published paper (misprint; Equation (10), Section 2.2): printed η_{t+h|t} = Σ_{j=1}^{h−1} c_j ε_{t+j} + ε_{t+h}
E2 [formal; foundation Kolmogorov probability axioms and multivariate normal likelihood theory] Analytical derivation of concentrated predictive log-likelihoods for MSE_h, MSCE, and the General Predictive Likelihood (GPL), proving that log GV = log s_1^2 + log |\hat{A}| and that |\hat{A}| = 1 under correct specification, and defining GTMSE via diagonal restriction on \hat{\Sigma} (Section 4 and Appendices C–E).
  S1 l_{GPL_h}(\theta, \hat{\Sigma} \mid Y) = -\frac{T-h}{2}\left(h \log(2\pi e) + \log|\hat{\Sigma}|\right), \quad GV = |\hat{\Sigma}| (by D7, C21; concentrating multivariate normal log-likelihood)

WARRANTS
W1 [abductive; premises C18] Climatologists expect the AR(1) parameter of temperature series to be positive. The GTMSE, MSCE and GPL estimates on the Greenland series are positive and similar to those reported in the climate literature (Hartmann et al. 2013; Cahill et al. 2015; Rahmstorf et al. 2017), while MSE_h pushes the parameter to a negative value; the authors read this as showing that the milder shrinkage is beneficial compared with the model estimated by MSE_1. The comparison with the literature is qualitative, on one series, and the MSE_1 estimate is positive as well.
W2 [inductive; premises C5, C14, C15, C16, C17, C22] When the model is correct or analogous to the true one, MSE_1 gives the least biased and most efficient estimates in the simulations, and the multi-step estimators over-shrink, especially on small samples and long horizons. Under misspecification the shrinkage removes redundant ETS parameters and the multi-step estimators forecast better on the two real series, so the choice depends on whether the parameters or the forecasts matter most.

LIMITATIONS
L1 [model_specification; affects C1, C8, C9, C10; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] In finite samples with large forecast horizons $h$, multi-step estimators can over-shrink parameters toward boundary values, introducing finite-sample estimation bias and slow convergence rates.
L2 [model_specification; affects C2; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The zero-shrinkage analytical result applies to static linear regression models without dynamic state transitions ($g = 0$) and does not transfer to dynamic regression models with autoregressive or moving-average error structures.
L3 [model_specification; affects C3; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The equivalence between General Predictive Likelihood and one-step-ahead MSE_1 relies on correct model specification so that $|\hat{A}| = 1$; under model misspecification, off-diagonal covariances do not cancel and GPL estimates diverge from MSE_1.
L4 [model_specification; affects C4; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The analytical derivation of GTMSE imposes a diagonal restriction setting off-diagonal forecast error covariances to zero, which does not hold in real dynamic time series processes whenever $c_j \ne 0$.
L5 [sample_size; affects C5, C14, C15, C22; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] Monte Carlo simulation results are restricted to two univariate data-generating processes (ETS(A,N,N) with $\alpha=0.2$ and ARIMA(0,1,1) with $\theta=0.6$) and pure additive linear models, and numerical outcomes are reported visually via boxplots rather than tabulated summary statistics.
L6 [sampling; affects C6, C16, C17, C18, C23; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] Empirical evaluations are limited to two individual univariate time series (Greenland Mean Surface Temperature and Air Passengers) with single fixed holdout origins rather than a large multi-series benchmark.
L7 [other; affects C8; could_reverse; mitigation none] The limit of ARIMA(1,1,1) under multi-step estimation is not φ̂ = θ̂ = −1: there the h-step variance factor equals h. Its minimum is at φ̂ = 0, θ̂ = −1, where every c_j is zero, a deterministic level rather than ARIMA(1,0,0). Found by the independent proof check.
L8 [other; affects C10; could_reverse; mitigation none] The switch at h > 2m rests on the misprinted coefficients of ETS(A,N,A) in Table 1; with the correct c_j = α + γ when j is a multiple of m, the pressure on α̂ always exceeds that on γ̂. Found by the independent proof check.
L9 [other; affects C11, C12; could_reverse; mitigation none] The efficiency comparisons are argued from the curvature of the likelihoods, not proved: under correct specification no consistent multi-step estimator has a lower asymptotic variance than the one-step Gaussian estimator, and a Monte Carlo with ETS(A,N,N) found MSCE more efficient than MSE_h. Found by the independent proof check.
L10 [other; affects C24; reduces_precision; mitigation none] TMSE is the maximum likelihood estimator when the forecast errors of all horizons are taken as independent with equal variances; what has no likelihood is TMSE with the variances the model implies, which grow with the horizon. Found by the independent proof check.
L11 [other; affects C26; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] MSCE shrinks the most only for small smoothing parameters: for ETS(A,N,N) with α = 0.7 and for ETS(A,A,N) with small α and β at long horizons, MSE_h shrinks more. Found by the independent proof check.
L12 [other; affects C3; reduces_precision; mitigation none] Under correct specification the General Predictive Likelihood approaches MSE_1 only when the sample is much longer than the horizon: it minimises the log-determinant of the residual autocovariance matrix, and in small samples it shrinks (ETS(A,N,N), α = 0.2, T = 50, h = 10: 63% of estimates at zero against 29% for MSE_1). Found by the second proof check.

OPEN QUESTIONS
O1 [from C1, C9] How the multi-step shrinkage mechanism extends to pure multiplicative state space exponential smoothing models remains only partially explored and is not analytically established across all multiplicative forms.
