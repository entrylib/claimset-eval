DEFINITIONS
D1 [variable] total flying insect biomass: Alcohol-wet mass of all insects caught by a Malaise trap, per day of exposure, weighed after draining through a 0.8 mm sieve.
D2 [method] Malaise trap sampling: Standardised bi-coloured Malaise traps (Townes design) run continuously from March to October and emptied about every 11 days; catches stored in 80% ethanol.
D3 [setting] German protected areas: 63 lowland locations in protected areas (Natura 2000 sites, nature reserves, landscape protection areas, water protection zones) in North Rhine-Westphalia (57), Brandenburg (5) and Rhineland-Palatinate (1), 1989-2016; 96 location-years, 1,503 samples. [identifiers wikidata:Q183]
D11 [organism] flying insects: All insects caught in flight by the traps, not identified further. [identifiers ncbitaxon:50557]
D4 [method] Bayesian hierarchical biomass model: Daily latent biomass log-normal with a location random effect and a log-linear annual trend; observed catches are sums of daily biomass over exposure intervals; fitted by MCMC in JAGS.
D5 [variable] annual decline rate: Percentage decrease of expected daily biomass per year, from the model's trend coefficient.
D6 [variable] seasonal decline over 27 years: Percentage decrease of biomass over 1989-2016, weighted over the season (April-October).
D7 [concept] weather, land use and habitat: Temperature, precipitation, wind, frost and winter rain; forest, agriculture, grassland and water cover within 200 m; plant richness and Ellenberg indicator values within 50 m.
D8 [setting] habitat cluster: Grouping of locations by vegetation: 1 nutrient-poor heathland, sandy grassland and dunes; 2 nutrient-rich grassland, margins and wasteland; 3 pioneer and shrub communities. [members D9, D10]
D9 [setting] nutrient-poor heathland, sandy grassland and dunes: Habitat cluster 1 (19 locations).
D10 [setting] nutrient-rich grassland, margins and wasteland: Habitat cluster 2 (41 locations).

CLAIMS
C1 [descriptive; principal; support: empirical_quant E1 (E1.R2, E1.R3); assumes C4; limitations L1, L2]
  In [[D3:German protected areas]], [[D1:total flying insect biomass]] of [[D11:flying insects]] declined by 76.7% over 27 years across the season (the [[D6:seasonal decline over 27 years]]), and by 81.6% in mid-summer.
C2 [associational; principal; support: empirical_quant E2; limitations L3]
  The decline of [[D1:total flying insect biomass]] is not explained by changes in [[D7:weather, land use and habitat]]: in [[D3:German protected areas]] the trend remains, and steepens, when they are in the model.
C4 [descriptive; supporting; assumption (methodological, unchecked)]
  Catches of a trap are representative of the flying insect biomass of its location in the sampling period.
C3 [descriptive; secondary; support: empirical_quant E1 (E1.R5, E1.R6)]
  The [[D5:annual decline rate]] was higher in [[D9:nutrient-poor heathland, sandy grassland and dunes]] than in [[D10:nutrient-rich grassland, margins and wasteland]], within [[D3:German protected areas]].

LIMITATIONS
L1 [sampling; affects C1; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation partial] Only 26 of 63 locations were sampled in more than one year, so the trend is estimated mostly across locations rather than within them.
L2 [sampling; affects C1; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The protected areas are small and 94% are enclosed by farmland, so they may act as sinks and the decline may not transfer to other landscapes.
L3 [measurement; affects C2; could_reverse; mitigation none] Land use was measured coarsely (proportions within 200 m) without management or pesticide use, and not every climate variable was analysed.
