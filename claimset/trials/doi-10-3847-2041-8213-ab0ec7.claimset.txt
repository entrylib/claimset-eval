DEFINITIONS
D1 [entity] M87*: The compact radio source at the centre of the galaxy M87.
D2 [entity] Event Horizon Telescope: A global very-long-baseline interferometer of radio telescopes observing at a wavelength of 1.3 mm.
D3 [data] 2017 EHT observations of M87: Interferometric data of M87 at 1.3 mm from the 2017 observing campaign of the Event Horizon Telescope.
D4 [method] image reconstruction: Reconstruction of images of M87* from the interferometric data by several independent methods and teams.
D5 [variable] ring diameter: Diameter of the bright emission ring in the reconstructed images. [unit uas]
D6 [theory] Kerr black hole shadow: The dark region and surrounding bright ring that general relativity predicts for a rotating (Kerr) black hole seen through the emission of nearby plasma.
D7 [method] GRMHD model comparison: Comparison of the data with a library of general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations of accretion flows, from which the black hole's mass is inferred.
D8 [variable] central mass: Mass of the compact object at the centre of M87, in solar masses. [unit solar masses]

CLAIMS
C1 [descriptive; principal; support: empirical_obs E1 (E1.R1, E1.R2, E1.R3)]
  Images of [[D1:M87*]] from the [[D2:Event Horizon Telescope]] show a bright ring, brighter in the south, with a [[D5:ring diameter]] of 42 ± 3 μas around a dark central region.
C2 [interpretive; principal; support: empirical_obs E1 (E1.R1), argument W1; uses C1]
  The size and shape of the ring around [[D1:M87*]] are consistent with the [[D6:Kerr black hole shadow]] that general relativity predicts.
C3 [descriptive; principal; support: empirical_quant E2 (E2.R1); uses C1]
  From the [[D7:GRMHD model comparison]], the [[D8:central mass]] of [[D1:M87*]] is (6.5 ± 0.7) × 10^9 solar masses.

WARRANTS
W1 [abductive] General relativity predicts a ring of a size set by the black hole's mass, around a darker region; a ring of that size and shape is what a Kerr black hole would show.

ARTEFACTS
A1 [figure, specimen] The source's image of M87* at 1.3 mm: a bright ring, brighter in the south, around a dark central region.
