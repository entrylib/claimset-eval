CLAIMSET Half Central Moment for Data Analysis
summary Moment measures of skewness and kurtosis are driven by the tails: one extreme value can dominate them. We propose shape coefficients built on the half central moment, the expected square root of the deviation from the mean. Negative deviations have imaginary roots, so its real part collects the deviations above the mean and its imaginary part those below. The Asymmetry coefficient, from the angle of this moment, lies strictly between $-1$ and $1$; it is determined by the probability on each side of the mean and by how evenly the deviations spread on each side, and it places the mean as an $L_{1.5}$-quantile. Hypotailedness, the absolute half moment scaled by the square root of the standard deviation, and the Extremity coefficient derived from it measure, as kurtosis does, how far the squared standardised deviations spread from one, but give values near the mean more weight and values in the tails less. We derive the influence functions, showing that they grow linearly for Asymmetry and quadratically for Hypotailedness. We also derive the sharp lower bound of the sample Hypotailedness, the asymptotic normality of the sample coefficients, and closed forms for seven standard distributions. In simulations we show that the sample Asymmetry is nearly unbiased, while Hypotailedness is biased upwards in small samples. Finally, on rock samples and four stock index return series, the coefficients change far less than moment skewness and kurtosis when the most extreme values are removed, and Extremity separates the rock variables best among tail-weight measures.

DEFINITIONS
D1 [variable] half central moment ($\mu_{0.5}$): The half central moment (HCM) of a variable $y$ with finite mean $\mu_1$: $$\mu_{0.5} = \mathrm{E}\left[(y - \mu_1)^{0.5}\right]$$, the square root taken as the principal root, so that $$\mu_{0.5} = \mathrm{E}\left[(y-\mu_1)_+^{0.5}\right] + i\,\mathrm{E}\left[(\mu_1-y)_+^{0.5}\right]$$: the real part $\mathcal{R}$ comes from the values above the mean and the imaginary part $\mathcal{I}$ from those below it. Sample estimate: $$m_{0.5} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} (y_j - m_1)^{0.5}$$, with $m_1$ the sample mean. [assumes C30]
D2 [variable] half absolute moment ($\nu_{0.5}$): The half absolute moment (HAM): $$\nu_{0.5} = \mathcal{R}(\mu_{0.5}) + \mathcal{I}(\mu_{0.5}) = \mathrm{E}|y - \mu_1|^{0.5}$$; sample estimate $$v_{0.5} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} |y_j - m_1|^{0.5}$$. [assumes C30]
D3 [variable] Asymmetry coefficient: $$\mathrm{Asymmetry} = 1 - \frac{4\phi}{\pi} = \frac{4}{\pi}\arctan\left(\frac{\mathcal{R}(\mu_{0.5}) - \mathcal{I}(\mu_{0.5})}{\nu_{0.5}}\right),$$ where $\phi = \mathrm{Arg}(\mu_{0.5}) \in \left[0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]$; positive when the part above the mean is the larger. The sample coefficient uses $m_{0.5}$ and $v_{0.5}$. [assumes C30]
D4 [variable] Hypotailedness: $\mathrm{Hypotailedness} = \frac{\nu_{0.5}}{\sigma^{0.5}}$ for a variable with finite positive variance $\sigma^2$; the sample coefficient divides $v_{0.5}$ by $\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j}(y_j - m_1)^2\right)^{0.25}$. [assumes C31]
D5 [variable] Extremity coefficient: $\mathrm{Extremity} = 2\,\mathrm{Hypotailedness}^{p} - 1$ with $p = \frac{\log 0.5}{\log(2 \times 3^{-0.75})} \approx 5.30$, so that the uniform distribution has Extremity 0. [assumes C31]
D6 [variable] Complex Hypotailedness ($\mathrm{CH}$): $\mathrm{CH} = \frac{\mu_{0.5}}{\sigma^{0.5}}$, the half central moment divided by the square root of the standard deviation (the manuscript of 19 November 2026). [assumes C31]
D7 [variable] one-sided Hypotailedness ($H_\pm$): For a variable with finite variance and deviation $D = y - \mu_1$: $$H_+ = \frac{\mathrm{E}\left[D^{0.5} \mid D > 0\right]}{\left(\mathrm{E}\left[D^2 \mid D > 0\right]\right)^{0.25}}$$ above the mean and $$H_- = \frac{\mathrm{E}\left[|D|^{0.5} \mid D < 0\right]}{\left(\mathrm{E}\left[D^2 \mid D < 0\right]\right)^{0.25}}$$ below it; in a sample, the same ratios over the values above and below the sample mean. [assumes C31]
D8 [variable] Complex Extremity ($\mathrm{CE}$): The bounded Complex Extremity proposed in this study: $\mathrm{CE} = \left(2H_+^{p} - 1\right) + i\left(2H_-^{p} - 1\right)$, with $H_\pm$ the one-sided Hypotailedness and $p = \frac{\log 0.5}{\log(2 \times 3^{-0.75})}$; the real part describes the values above the mean. [assumes C31]
D9 [variable] moment skewness: The standardised third central moment $\frac{\mathrm{E}[(y-\mu_1)^3]}{\sigma^3}$; sample version with $\frac{1}{n}$ moments. [source B27]
D10 [variable] excess kurtosis: The standardised fourth central moment minus 3, $\frac{\mathrm{E}[(y-\mu_1)^4]}{\sigma^4} - 3$; sample version with $\frac{1}{n}$ moments. [source B28]
D11 [variable] L-skewness: The L-moment ratio $\tau_3 = \frac{\lambda_3}{\lambda_2}$; sample version from unbiased probability-weighted moments. [source B10]
D12 [variable] L-kurtosis: The L-moment ratio $\tau_4 = \frac{\lambda_4}{\lambda_2}$; sample version from unbiased probability-weighted moments. [source B10]
D13 [variable] TL-skewness: The trimmed L-moment ratio $\tau_3^{(1)}$ with trimming (1,1), which exists without moments. [source B11]
D14 [variable] TL-kurtosis: The trimmed L-moment ratio $\tau_4^{(1)}$ with trimming (1,1). [source B11]
D15 [variable] quartile skewness: Bowley's quartile skewness $\frac{Q_3 + Q_1 - 2Q_2}{Q_3 - Q_1}$, the member at $u = \frac{1}{4}$ of the quantile skewness family of Groeneveld & Meeden (1984); sample quartiles by linear interpolation (R type 7). [source B29]
D16 [variable] medcouple: The medcouple, a robust skewness measure in $[-1, 1]$ built from the median of a kernel over pairs of values on either side of the median. [source B15]
D17 [variable] mean-based skewness: Skewness centred at the mean, $2F(\mu_1) - 1$ ($F$ the distribution function), bounded in $[-1, 1]$ and positive for right-skewed distributions, the same sign convention as moment skewness; the bounded form used by Eberl & Klar (2020) of Tajuddin's (1996) log-odds $\log\frac{F(\mu_1)}{1 - F(\mu_1)}$. Sample version $2\hat F(m_1) - 1$, the share of values at or below the sample mean, rescaled. [source B8]
D18 [variable] octile kurtosis: Moors octile kurtosis $\frac{(E_7 - E_5) + (E_3 - E_1)}{E_6 - E_2}$, $E_k$ the $k$-th octile; sample octiles by R type 7. [source B16]
D19 [variable] Geary ratio: The ratio of the mean absolute deviation to the standard deviation, $\frac{\mathrm{E}|y-\mu_1|}{\sigma}$, the order-one analogue of Hypotailedness. [source B4]
D20 [variable] half-moment coefficients: The coefficients built on the half central moment that the study compares with other measures: [[D3:Asymmetry coefficient]], [[D4:Hypotailedness]], [[D5:Extremity coefficient]] and [[D8:Complex Extremity]].
D21 [variable] comparison measures: The measures the half-moment coefficients are compared with: for skewness, [[D9:moment skewness]], [[D11:L-skewness]], [[D13:TL-skewness]], [[D15:quartile skewness]], [[D16:medcouple]] and [[D17:mean-based skewness]]; for tail weight, [[D10:excess kurtosis]], [[D12:L-kurtosis]], [[D14:TL-kurtosis]], [[D18:octile kurtosis]] and [[D19:Geary ratio]].
D22 [design] single-outlier contamination: Samples of size $n$ drawn from a base distribution with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$; in each, the first value is replaced by $\mu + k\sigma$. Every measure is computed on the clean sample and on the contaminated one.
D23 [variable] standardised shift: For one measure, base distribution, $n$ and $k$: the mean over replications of the measure on the contaminated samples minus its mean on the clean samples, divided by the standard deviation of the measure over the clean samples.
D24 [data] petroleum rock samples: Measurements on 48 rock samples from a petroleum reservoir (BP research), the rock data set of R's datasets package: area, perimeter, shape and permeability. [source B1]
D25 [data] European stock index returns: Daily closing prices of four European stock indices (DAX, SMI, CAC, FTSE), 1991-1998, business days, 1860 observations each: the EuStockMarkets data set of R's datasets package; analysed as daily log returns (1859 each). [source B2]
D26 [design] expanding window: Every measure computed on the first $t$ returns of a series, for every $t$ from 30 to the full length.
D27 [variable] standardised change on removing the most extreme values: For one measure and data set: the value without the k observations farthest from the mean minus the value on all observations, divided by the bootstrap standard error of the value on all observations.
D28 [variable] discrimination ratio: For one measure and group of four data sets (the rock variables, or the index return series): the standard deviation of the measure over the four data sets divided by the root mean square of their bootstrap standard errors; how far apart the measure places the data sets relative to its own sampling noise.
D29 [variable] spike ratio: For one measure and index: the largest change of the measure between consecutive rolling windows of 260 returns (step one return), divided by the standard deviation of the measure over all windows.
D30 [variable] roughness: For one measure and index: the median change of the measure between consecutive rolling windows of 260 returns, divided by the standard deviation of the measure over all windows.

C5 "What Asymmetry measures: probability and spread on each side of the mean" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E32, simulation E5 (E5.R1); assumes C30; uses C4; limitations L1; verification agent_checked; planning confirmatory_with_deviations; outcome supported]
  For a variable $y$ with a finite mean $\mu_1$ and positive probability on both sides of it, let $D = y - \mu_1$, $p_+ = P(D > 0)$, $p_- = P(D < 0)$, and $$h_+ = \frac{\mathrm{E}\left[D^{0.5} \mid D > 0\right]}{\left(\mathrm{E}\left[D \mid D > 0\right]\right)^{0.5}}, \qquad h_- = \frac{\mathrm{E}\left[(-D)^{0.5} \mid D < 0\right]}{\left(\mathrm{E}\left[-D \mid D < 0\right]\right)^{0.5}}.$$ Then $h_+, h_- \in (0, 1]$, each equal to 1 exactly when the deviations on its side are constant, and the parts of the [[D1:half central moment]] satisfy $$\frac{\mathcal{R}(\mu_{0.5})}{\mathcal{I}(\mu_{0.5})} = \sqrt{\frac{p_+}{p_-}}\;\frac{h_+}{h_-},$$ so the [[D3:Asymmetry coefficient]] is determined by the probability on each side of the mean and by how evenly the deviations are spread on each side.
  in plain words: The Asymmetry coefficient compares how much probability lies on each side of the mean and how evenly the deviations are spread on each side. A long right tail lowers both on the upper side, so Asymmetry is negative for right-skewed distributions.
  extends B19: Fishburn's (1977) partial moments of order $\alpha$ below a target; with the mean as target and $\alpha = 0.5$ the lower one is the imaginary part of the half central moment.

C6 "Asymmetry locates the mean as an L1.5-quantile" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E33, simulation E6 (E6.R1); assumes C30; uses C4; limitations L1; verification agent_checked; planning confirmatory_with_deviations; outcome supported]

C10 "What Hypotailedness and Extremity measure: the spread of squared standardised deviations" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E46, simulation E19 (E19.R1); assumes C31; uses C7, C9; verification agent_checked; planning confirmatory; outcome supported]
  For a variable with a finite positive variance $\sigma^2$, let $V = \frac{(y - \mu_1)^2}{\sigma^2}$, the squared standardised deviation, whose mean is 1, and $g(v) = \frac{3 + v}{4} - v^{0.25}$. Then one minus the [[D4:Hypotailedness]] equals $\mathrm{E}[g(V)]$, the [[D5:Extremity coefficient]] equals $2(1 - \mathrm{E}[g(V)])^p - 1$ and so decreases as $\mathrm{E}[g(V)]$ grows, and, with a finite fourth moment, the [[D10:excess kurtosis]] plus 2 equals $\mathrm{E}[(V - 1)^2]$. The function $g$ is strictly convex and zero only at $v = 1$, is $\frac{3}{32}(v - 1)^2 + O(|v - 1|^3)$ near 1, is $\frac{3}{4}$ at $v = 0$, and is $\frac{v}{4} + O(v^{0.25})$ for large $v$. So Hypotailedness, Extremity and kurtosis all measure how far $V$ spreads from 1: Hypotailedness through the loss $g$, kurtosis through the loss $(v - 1)^2$. Scaled by $\frac{32}{3}$, so that it agrees with $(v - 1)^2$ to second order at $v = 1$, the loss $g$ gives a value at the mean 8 times the weight the kurtosis loss gives it, and a value far out a weight linear rather than quadratic in $V$.
  in plain words: Hypotailedness and Extremity measure, as kurtosis does, how far the squared standardised deviations spread from their average of 1: how far the probability lies from one standard deviation either side of the mean. Compared with kurtosis they weigh values near the mean more and values in the tails less.
  extends B33: Moors (1986) reads kurtosis as the variance of the squared standardised deviation, the spread of the probability about the mean plus and minus one standard deviation; Hypotailedness measures the same spread with a loss that weighs the centre more and the tails less.
  extends B3: Geary's (1947) ratio $a(0.5)$, the sample Hypotailedness, is the mean fourth root of the squared standardised deviation.

C20 "Influence functions: linear for Asymmetry, quadratic for Hypotailedness" [formal; principal; extension; support: formal_proof E37, simulation E17 (E17.R1); assumes C31; uses C4, C7; limitations L1; inherits C30; verification agent_checked; planning confirmatory_with_deviations; outcome supported]
  At a distribution with a finite fourth moment and a density bounded near its mean, the influence function of the [[D3:Asymmetry coefficient]] at an outlying value $x$ is unbounded and linear in $x$ for large $|x|$: it tends to $-\infty$ as $x \to +\infty$ and to $+\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$; the influence functions of [[D4:Hypotailedness]] and of the [[D5:Extremity coefficient]] tend to $-\infty$ quadratically in $x$ as $|x| \to \infty$, through the variance.
  in plain words: A single extreme value moves the Asymmetry coefficient in proportion to its distance from the mean, as it moves L-skewness, but moves Hypotailedness and Extremity in proportion to the squared distance, through the variance.
  extends B17: The influence function of kurtosis is a quartic in the outlying value.
  extends B15: The medcouple has a bounded influence function.

LIMITATIONS
L1 [deviation; affects C1, C2, C3, C5, C6, C11, C12, C20; reduces_precision; mitigation none] Run RN6 departed from plan PL1 in the numerical check of the influence functions only: the central difference $\frac{T(F_\varepsilon) - T(F_{-\varepsilon})}{2\varepsilon}$ with $\varepsilon = \frac{10^{-6}}{1 + k^2}$ replaced the one-sided difference with $\varepsilon = 10^{-6}$, and the error of an influence-function value was measured against $\max(|\text{value}|, 1)$, because the one-sided difference is too coarse for the tolerance $10^{-8}$ and the influence functions cross zero; the proofs and the other checks followed the plan.
L12 [sampling; affects C21, C23; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The robustness and sampling studies use three base distributions (normal, Student t(5), exponential) and one kind of contamination, a single value moved along the line mean + k standard deviations.
L13 [other; affects C24, C25, C26, C27, C28, C29; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The real-data results describe two data sets, 48 rock samples and the 1991-1998 returns of four indices; whether the coefficients are easier to interpret than skewness and kurtosis is not tested.
L14 [other; affects C25, C26, C27, C28, C29; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The comparison of robustness and precision uses two groups of four data sets and summarises each group by medians; the discrimination ratio depends on which data sets are grouped together.
