CLAIMSET Claude Sonnet and Claude Haiku agents keeping Claimset lab records and completing claimsets: a pilot on one simulation study
summary Can smaller AI models run a research project with a Claimset lab record and write it up as a claimset? Two Claude Sonnet and two Claude Haiku agents each did one simulation study, comparing how well two Python packages estimate exponential smoothing parameters; four more agents completed a claimset from one of the resulting records. All four lab records passed the tool's own verification and all four claimsets were valid. The Sonnet agents planned their tests before seeing data; the Haiku agents did not. No claim was wrong, but no claimset stated the study's main findings: the lab format turns only pass-or-fail hypotheses into claims, so the agents tested thresholds they chose themselves instead of reporting estimates. EntryLib's scoring rubric ranked a claimset without planned tests, and one without limitations or scope, above more careful ones. These are exploratory results from two runs per model on one task.

DEFINITIONS
D1 [method] claimset-lab: The claimset-lab skill (`.claude/skills/claimset-lab/SKILL.md`, installed by `claimset init --agent claude`) and the `claimset lab` commands of claimset 0.41.6, which keep a lab record (lab record specification v0.21): an append-only, hash-chained log of questions, hypotheses, plans, approvals, runs, results and verdicts, kept on a study branch and pushed after every command.
D2 [method] claimset-submit: The claimset-submit skill of claimset 0.41.6 (claimset specification v0.41), together with `claimset lab export`, `claimset validate` and `claimset render`: it turns a lab record into a claimset and completes it for submission.
D3 [method] Sonnet agent: A Claude Code 2.1.322 subagent started with the Agent tool (type general-purpose) on the model claude-sonnet-5-5, working in a folder of its own that holds the study brief, `AGENTS.md` and the claimset skills as files; it asks the human by ending its turn with its questions. [part of D5]
D4 [method] Haiku agent: The same as the [[D3:Sonnet agent]], on the model claude-haiku-5-5. [part of D5]
D5 [method] agents under test: The two agent configurations compared: the [[D3:Sonnet agent]] and the [[D4:Haiku agent]]. [members D3, D4]
D6 [setting] ETS estimation brief: The research task given to every agent (`study/brief.md`): compare how well smooth 1.2.0 (`ES`) and statsforecast 2.1.1 (`AutoETS` with a fixed model form) estimate the smoothing parameters of the ETS model that generated simulated data, by bias, RMSE and MAE and by whether the RMSE falls as the series length grows; five processes (ETS(A,N,N) with α 0.1 and 0.5, ETS(A,A,N) with (α, β) (0.3, 0.05) and (0.5, 0.2), ETS(A,N,A) with m = 12 and (α, γ) (0.2, 0.1)), series lengths 50, 100, 250 and 500, 300 replicates per cell, smooth's default backcasting as a separate arm, failures counted.
D7 [design] lab phase: Phase 1: four runs, two of the [[D3:Sonnet agent]] and two of the [[D4:Haiku agent]], each in its own git repository with a local remote, given the same task ("Do the study in brief.md with claimset-lab, following its skill, from the first question to the finished record. Ask the human anything you need through me."). The orchestrating agent relays questions and answers them only from [[D9:the fixed answers]], gives no other help, and never shows an agent another run or [[D10:the answer key]]. Runs in parallel on one 4-core machine.
D8 [design] claimset phase: Phase 2: the lab record of run lab-sonnet-1 (the only one that verifies, planned before its data and fixed statsforecast's model form) copied to four folders, with the draft its agent had exported removed; two [[D3:Sonnet agent]] and two [[D4:Haiku agent]] runs, each told "Export this lab record with `claimset lab export` and complete the claimset for submission, following the claimset-submit skill. Do not submit it." Questions answered as in [[D7:the lab phase]].
D9 [design] fixed answers: The answers the orchestrating agent gives in place of the human, the same to every run (`study/human.md`): name and ORCID iD; licence CC-BY-4.0; funding none; the competing interest of the brief; "Approved." for a plan that names its quantities and decision rule before the data are run, otherwise "Please state the quantities and the decision rule first."; for highlights "Choose up to three principal claims you consider most important."; for anything else "Use your judgement and say what you decided." [part of D7, D8]
D10 [data] answer key: The findings a correct paper on [[D6:the brief]] would state, with their numbers (`reference/answer_key.md`), from a reference simulation of the full design written by the orchestrating agent before Phase 1 without claimset-lab (`reference/simulate.py`, `optcheck.py`, `damped_check.py`). Amended once during Phase 1 (statsforecast's β cap).
D11 [variable] record verifies: Whether `claimset lab verify` reports the lab record OK at the end of a run (hash chain, rules, run files).
D12 [variable] tool refusals: Number of commands that `claimset` refused in a run with a lab rule (L1–L30) or an argument or overwrite error; validator errors on a claimset are counted separately.
D13 [variable] plans before data: Whether each confirmatory run of a lab record ran under a plan with its decision rule recorded, and approved by the human, before the run, and whether its hypotheses were written before any run informed them: `all`, `all, hypotheses after pilots` or `none` (no plan).
D14 [variable] model form fixed: Whether a run's simulation script fixed statsforecast's model form as the brief asks (`damped=False`); without it, `AutoETS(model="AAN")` and `ets_f(..., model="AAN")` choose between ETS(A,A,N) and ETS(A,Ad,N).
D15 [variable] convergence checked: Whether a run's script read any optimiser status (for statsforecast, the iteration count `model_["fit"].nit`) and counted fits that did not converge.
D16 [variable] output tokens: Model output tokens of an agent over its whole run, summed from the usage of every model call in its transcript. [unit 1]
D17 [variable] active time: Time an agent spent working in a run, excluding waits for answers, as reported by the harness. [unit min]
D18 [variable] record anchored: Whether the agent anchored the finished record (`claimset lab anchor`).
D19 [variable] runs written as the human: Number of run events an agent recorded with `--by` the human's actor ID.
D20 [variable] claimset validates: Whether `claimset validate` reports the final claimset valid (no errors).
D21 [variable] validator outcomes until valid: The position, among the validator outcomes a claimset run saw (the export's own summary included), of the first with no errors. [unit 1]
D22 [variable] warnings left: Validator warnings on the final claimset. [unit 1]
D23 [method] EntryLib rubric 0.2: The scoring rubric EntryLib applies to published claimsets (entrylib-rubric 0.2.0), run on this machine with `claimset score` and without `--novelty`: each principal claim starts at 100 and loses fixed costs for its problems (support, plan, checkability, context, open limitations and assumptions); the paper's score is that of its lowest principal claim.
D24 [variable] reliability score: The paper score of a claimset under [[D23:EntryLib rubric 0.2]], 0–100. [unit 1]
D25 [variable] claim correctness: Each claim of a claimset checked against [[D10:the answer key]]: `correct` (direction and magnitude agree within Monte Carlo error), `partly correct` (direction agrees; magnitude, scope or a qualifier does not), `wrong`, or `not supported by the record`.
D26 [variable] answer-key findings stated: Number of the answer key's findings on bias (K2–K5), statsforecast's non-convergence (K1.2) and statsforecast's β cap (K8) that a claimset states as claims. [unit 1]
D27 [variable] principal claims with scope: Number of a claimset's principal claims that have a scope. [unit 1]
D28 [variable] limitations: Number of limitations a claimset records. [unit 1]
D29 [variable] research agent's model kept: Whether a completed claimset keeps the model of the agent that did the research, as the lab record names it.
D30 [variable] interventions: Number of messages the orchestrating agent sent a run that were not answers from [[D9:the fixed answers]]. [unit 1]
D31 [design] reference simulation: The reference analysis behind [[D10:the answer key]]: 300 series per cell of [[D6:the brief]], each fitted by smooth with optimised initial states, smooth with backcasting and statsforecast `AutoETS(model, season_length, damped=False)` with its defaults (likelihood, bounds `both`), seeds `numpy.random.default_rng([20261111, process, T, replicate])`.
D32 [concept] statsforecast β cap: In statsforecast 2.1.1 (`statsforecast.ets.initparam`), when α is estimated its starting value is $$\alpha_0 = 0.0001 + 0.2 \cdot \frac{0.9999 - 0.0001}{m}$$ (0.20006 for $m = 1$), and the upper bound of β is set to $\min(0.9999, \alpha_0)$ for the whole optimisation, so β cannot exceed $\alpha_0$.
D33 [variable] decision thresholds: The thresholds a lab record's confirmatory plans fix in their decision rules, as the plans state them; `none` when a record has no plan.
D34 [variable] hypotheses about an estimate: Number of a lab record's hypotheses that state the direction or size of an estimate (a bias towards zero, an estimate on a bound, a bias of about −0.04) rather than a pass-or-fail threshold. [unit 1]
D35 [variable] rubric cost: Points that [[D23:EntryLib rubric 0.2]] deducts from a claim's score for one problem, as its file rubric.json states them. [unit 1]
D36 [design] reading of the rubric's code: The orchestrating agent read the costs in src/rubric/rubric.json and the rule that applies the plan check in src/rubric/score.py (lines 28 and 111), at the commit artefact A10 names.
D37 [variable] statsforecast iteration cap: Number of statsforecast fits whose optimiser stopped at its iteration cap (`nit` = 1001) in [[D31:the reference simulation]]. [unit 1]

WARRANTS
W1 [inductive; premises C12] The claimset-lab skill offers one route from a run to a claim: a hypothesis decided by a rule, with computed rules preferred whenever the outcome can be computed from numbers (SKILL.md, lines 83–100), and export makes claims only from hypotheses with a verdict or a final status (lab record v0.21 §9). Every planned run accordingly chose thresholds the brief did not give (an RMSE ratio within 10 %, |bias| at most 0.02, shares of at least 0.75, at least 12 violations), and its claims are the verdicts on them; the one run without a plan wrote notes and claims about the estimates themselves (the γ pile-up, statsforecast's seasonal α bias, the β cap).

LIMITATIONS
L1 [sample_size; affects C2, C3, C4, C8, C9, C17, C18; could_reverse; mitigation none] Two runs per agent and phase: differences between the Sonnet and Haiku agents rest on two runs each and could reverse with more runs.
L2 [confounding; affects C1, C2, C3, C5, C9; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] The agents ran as subagents of the orchestrating session: they read the skills as files, the claimset guard hook of their folders was not active, and they received the orchestrating session's own project instructions (which led one Haiku run to ask its questions one at a time); four runs shared one 4-core machine.
L3 [measurement; affects C6, C7; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation partial] One scorer, the orchestrating agent, wrote the answer key and the protocol and scored every claim, knowing which agent wrote it.
L4 [deviation; affects C6, C7, C20; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] The answer key was amended once during the lab phase, after a Haiku run reported statsforecast's β cap; the claimsets were scored against the amended key.
L5 [sampling; affects C1, C2, C3, C4, C15, C5, C6, C7, C8, C9, C10, C11; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] One study task (an ETS simulation comparison in Python), one version of claimset (0.41.6) and of its skills, one harness (Claude Code 2.1.322).
L6 [deviation; affects C5; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] One claimset run (claimset-haiku-2) stopped with its own stated work undone and was sent one message with no content ("Continue.") before it finished.

OPEN QUESTIONS
O1 [from C2, C3, C9, C20] Whether the differences between the Sonnet and Haiku agents hold over more runs, other study tasks and agents in sessions of their own is not established.
O2 [from C17, C18] Whether refusing writes by a human actor in agent harnesses, and keeping the research agent's model through export, would prevent the misattributions of a run and of a study's research is not tested.
O3 [from C4, C15] Whether a skill instruction to report each fit's model form and optimiser status as results would close the gaps in fixing the model form and checking convergence is not tested.
O4 [from C7, C8] Whether a plan that fixes estimates rather than a decision rule would lead agents to state the brief's findings as claims is not tested.
