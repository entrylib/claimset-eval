DEFINITIONS
D1 [method] smooth ES, optimised initials: smooth 1.2.0 ES(model=<form>, initial='optimal', loss='likelihood', bounds='usual'); smoothing parameters estimated by maximum likelihood with the initial states estimated jointly. It estimates the smoothing parameters of the ETS model of [[D4:the ETS data generating processes]] fitted in the generating form.
D2 [method] smooth ES, backcasting: smooth 1.2.0 ES(model=<form>, initial='backcasting', loss='likelihood', bounds='usual'): smooth's default; initial states obtained by backcasting, only smoothing parameters optimised. It estimates the smoothing parameters of the ETS model of [[D4:the ETS data generating processes]] fitted in the generating form.
D3 [method] statsforecast AutoETS: statsforecast 2.1.1 AutoETS(season_length=m, model=<form>, damped=False) with the form fixed to the generating one (no selection); maximum likelihood with optimised initial states; bounds lower 1e-4, upper 0.9999 for alpha, beta, gamma, beta<=alpha, gamma<=1-alpha, and admissibility. It estimates the smoothing parameters of the ETS model of [[D4:the ETS data generating processes]] fitted in the generating form.
D4 [data] ETS data generating processes: Series simulated from the innovations (single source of error) form of ETS with additive Gaussian errors $\varepsilon_t \sim N(0, 1)$ independent, started from $l_0 = 100$, $b_0 = 1$ and seasonal indices $s_j = 10\sin(2\pi j/12)$, $j = 0, \dots, 11$, without burn-in. ETS(A,N,N): $$\begin{aligned} y_t &= l_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t \\ l_t &= l_{t-1} + \alpha \varepsilon_t \end{aligned}$$ ETS(A,A,N): $$\begin{aligned} y_t &= l_{t-1} + b_{t-1} + \varepsilon_t \\ l_t &= l_{t-1} + b_{t-1} + \alpha \varepsilon_t \\ b_t &= b_{t-1} + \beta \varepsilon_t \end{aligned}$$ ETS(A,N,A) with period $m = 12$: $$\begin{aligned} y_t &= l_{t-1} + s_{t-m} + \varepsilon_t \\ l_t &= l_{t-1} + \alpha \varepsilon_t \\ s_t &= s_{t-m} + \gamma \varepsilon_t \end{aligned}$$ Parameter settings: ETS(A,N,N) with $\alpha = 0.1$ and $\alpha = 0.5$; ETS(A,A,N) with $(\alpha, \beta) = (0.3, 0.05)$ and $(0.5, 0.2)$; ETS(A,N,A) with $(\alpha, \gamma) = (0.2, 0.1)$; together 8 parameter-setting cells per length. Lengths $T \in \{50, 100, 250, 500\}$; 300 replicates per process and length.
D5 [variable] bias: Mean over successful replicates of (estimate minus true value) of a smoothing parameter.
D6 [variable] RMSE: Square root of the mean over successful replicates of the squared (estimate minus true value) of a smoothing parameter.
D7 [variable] MAE: Mean over successful replicates of the absolute value of (estimate minus true value) of a smoothing parameter.
D8 [concept] consistency: The RMSE of an estimator falls towards zero as series length T grows; here assessed by comparing the RMSE at T=500 with the RMSE at T=50 in each parameter-setting cell.
D9 [variable] estimates on a bound: Number of smoothing parameter estimates within 0.001 of a bound of the parameter space: $\alpha$ at 0 or 1, $\beta$ at 0 or $\alpha$, $\gamma$ at 0 or $1-\alpha$. Such estimates are included in bias, RMSE and MAE.

C4 [descriptive; supporting; assumption (methodological, unchecked)]
  Both [[D1:smooth ES]] and [[D3:statsforecast AutoETS]] maximise the Gaussian likelihood of the innovations form of ETS (smooth loss='likelihood', statsforecast opt_crit='lik'), so the two differ in optimiser, parameter bounds and treatment of initial states and not in the objective.
C5 [descriptive; supporting; assumption (methodological, unchecked)]
  The smoothing parameters $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ have the same meaning in [[D1:smooth ES]] and [[D3:statsforecast AutoETS]], the innovations form with $b_t = b_{t-1} + \beta \varepsilon_t$ and $s_t = s_{t-m} + \gamma \varepsilon_t$, so that their estimates are compared with the true values without conversion.

EVIDENCE
E1 [quant; design other] Simulation of the ETS data generating processes of [[D4]] and estimation of the smoothing parameters by three arms on the same series: smooth ES with optimised initial states, smooth ES with backcasting, and statsforecast AutoETS, each with the generating model form fixed and no model selection. Five processes, four series lengths and 300 replicates per cell (seed 20261111; series drawn with numpy default_rng(SeedSequence([20261111, process index, T]))), 6000 series fitted by each arm, 9600 successful parameter estimates per arm; no fit failed. Per arm, process, parameter and length the bias, RMSE and MAE of the estimate minus the true value and the number of estimates on a bound are recorded in the data artefact. The three decision quantities of the plan are the minimum and maximum over the 32 cells of the ratio of the RMSE of smooth with optimised initial states to the RMSE of statsforecast, the number of the 8 cells at T=50 where smooth with backcasting has a lower RMSE than smooth with optimised initial states, and per arm the number of the 8 cells where the RMSE at T=500 is not below the RMSE at T=50. They are descriptive summaries of simulation output under fixed seeds; no inferential test is applied.

LIMITATIONS
L1 [data_quality; affects C1, C2, C3; could_bias_magnitude; mitigation none] Estimates on a bound of the parameter space are included in the bias, RMSE and MAE: of 9600 estimates per arm, 1891 (smooth ES with backcasting), 1218 (smooth ES with optimised initial states) and 1191 (statsforecast AutoETS) are on a bound, 1159, 588 and 314 of them for gamma. The bias, RMSE and MAE of each arm therefore partly reflect estimates at the edge of the parameter space.
L2 [sample_size; affects C3; reduces_precision; mitigation none] The consistency claim compares the RMSE at the shortest and longest series only, in the 8 cells; intermediate lengths and the rate of decrease are recorded in the data artefact and are not assessed.
L3 [model_specification; affects C1, C2, C3; reduces_transferability; mitigation none] The comparison uses one set of parameter values per process and a single seed; results for other parameter values, error distributions or initial states may differ.
